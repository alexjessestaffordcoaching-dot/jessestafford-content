## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader clears the obstacle that makes someone else's visible win possible, then watches the credit for it go entirely to the person who shipped, because the enabling work left no artifact behind.
**Context box text:** YOU CLEARED THE PATH. SOMEONE ELSE GOT THE CREDIT.
**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is systems and accountability content, not a warm connection story. Jesse's expression is clear-eyed and matter-of-fact, not upset, not smiling. Someone naming a mechanism he's seen play out many times.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A hidden gear mechanism, set into the floor or lower half of the frame, mechanically connected up to a raised, illuminated platform or spotlight above it. The gears do the work. The platform gets the light.
**First-second read:** The viewer sees a bright, elevated platform under a spotlight at the top of the frame, and a smaller figure crouched among exposed gears in the shadowed lower half, clearly the thing making the platform turn, clearly not standing in the light himself.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse is small and positioned low and slightly off-center, at the gear mechanism, looking upward toward the lit platform. He is not on the platform. Scale contrast between the modest gear-work he's tending and the large, bright platform above does the storytelling.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Vertical emphasis within a 16:9 frame. The upper third of the frame contains a raised, brightly lit circular platform, empty except for implied importance (a single beam of light striking it from above, as if something valuable sits or will sit there). The lower two-thirds contains an exposed gear mechanism built into the floor, charcoal linework gears of varying sizes turning against each other, with visible connecting rods running up toward the platform above. Jesse is positioned in the lower-left third of the frame, crouched beside the largest gear, one hand resting on it, his body small relative to the platform above. He is looking up and to the right, toward the lit platform, not at his own hands. Significant negative space separates him from the platform. He is clearly the reason the platform is lit. He is not standing in the light.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. The lit platform above may carry a warm pale-yellow watercolor wash to read as "illuminated," kept subordinate to the blue/white/charcoal triad.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Crouched beside the largest gear, one hand resting on it, weight forward, looking up and to the right toward the lit platform. Expression is direct and focused, clear-eyed, not upset, the look of someone naming a pattern he's watched happen many times, not reacting to it in the moment.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The frame is split between a shadowed mechanical underlayer and a bright, elevated platform above it. Charcoal-line gears of different sizes mesh together in the lower two-thirds, connected by visible rods and belts running upward out of frame toward the platform. The platform itself is simple and largely empty, defined mainly by the light striking it, implying whatever gets placed there will be seen and credited. Jesse tends the largest gear at floor level, small in the frame relative to the platform above. One clear focal relationship: the gears below power the platform above, and the distance between them is the entire story. Negative space surrounds Jesse on both sides so he reads as isolated with the mechanism, not part of a crowd.

CONTEXT BOX: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: YOU CLEARED THE PATH. SOMEONE ELSE GOT THE CREDIT.

TONE GUARDRAILS: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene — it does not compete with it.

Jesse stands just behind and to the side, having done the enabling work that made the demo possible, visibly not part of the moment about to happen.
**First-second read:** The viewer sees one person stepping forward, energized, about to present, while a second person just behind them stands still, arms loosely at his sides, clearly not part of what's about to be shown.
**Compositional approach:** The colleague is positioned slightly ahead and toward the center-right of the frame, body angled forward as if about to address someone off-frame. Jesse stands behind and to the left, at a slight distance, same eye line, watching. Not hierarchical, not confrontational — parallel positioning with a clear forward/behind gap that reads as "the one who gets seen" and "the one who does not."

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale inversion. A single, small, unremarkable gear, dwarfed by an enormous, ornately detailed trophy-shaped machine it is quietly powering. The mismatch in size between the thing doing the work and the thing getting the glory makes the point without needing any text beyond the context box.
**First-second read:** The viewer sees one giant, ornate, trophy-like machine dominating most of the frame, running and gleaming, and only after a beat notices the small figure crouched at its base, adjusting the one small gear that is actually turning the whole thing.
**Why it works:** It makes the size mismatch between invisible enabling work and visible credit literal and slightly absurd, which is exactly the gap the post argues nobody names out loud. The tone stays direct and focused, same as the other two concepts, since the irony here is structural, not comedic.

In the lower-left of the frame, small in scale by comparison, Jesse crouches at the base of the giant structure, adjusting one modest, unremarkable gear that is the only thing visibly connected to the machine's base and driving its movement. The size gap between the small gear and the enormous trophy-machine is the entire point of the image.
