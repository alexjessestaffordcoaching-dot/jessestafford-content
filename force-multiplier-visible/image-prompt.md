## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** Jesse spent two years doing the highest-leverage work of his career for a six-person team, never left a record of it, and when he moved on nobody picked it up because nobody had ever seen it happen.
**Context box text:** YOU CLEARED THE PATH. SOMEONE ELSE GOT THE CREDIT.
**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is visibility and career-positioning content. Jesse's expression is level and unsentimental — someone naming a cost he paid himself, not complaining about it.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** The log that was never kept. The article is explicit: he cannot recall the projects he unblocked in any given quarter unless he wrote them down at the time, and a one-line entry once a week would have become his entire calibration case. The physical form is a maintenance logbook on a chain beside a working machine — most of its pages blank while the machine runs fine because of work nobody recorded.
**Setting:** A real building plant room — a service corridor with a running machine, a wall-mounted logbook station, and a work light.
**First-second read:** A man standing at a logbook whose pages are empty, while behind him a large machine runs perfectly.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse mid-left at the logbook station at human scale, the running machine filling the right two-thirds behind him.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important:** A real building plant room, seen wide. On the left, a wall-mounted logbook station at chest height: an open logbook on a hinged board, a pen hanging on a chain beside it. Jesse stands at it in three-quarter view at ordinary human scale, one hand flat on the open page, thumbing back through the previous pages with the other — and every page he has turned back is blank. Filling the right two-thirds behind him, a large service machine runs smoothly, well maintained, clearly working. Jesse is turned away from the machine and toward the empty pages. The machine is the largest thing in the frame and the empty logbook is the smallest, and the gap between them is the point. Keep the upper-left area above the logbook as open white space.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important:** The service machine has an open inspection panel showing a drive gear train of four meshed cogs, a leather drive belt running over two pulleys, a governor with spinning weights on a spindle, and a needle-dial pressure gauge reading steady in the middle of its range. Its base sits on adjustable threaded feet with visible lock nuts. The logbook board hangs on an exposed piano hinge with a visible pin, and the pen chain runs through a small eyelet plate. A wall-mounted valve beside it has a hand-wheel with a knurled rim and an exposed stem thread. In the negative space — the open upper-left area above the logbook and the margin down the left edge — sits the charcoal schematic layer: unpainted linework of a service-record diagram, a ruled column of entry rows with every row left empty. The S mark is cast into the eyelet plate of the logbook board.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Here: standing at the logbook station in three-quarter view, one hand flat on the open page, the other lifting a turned-back page, head down looking at the blank sheets, expression level and unsentimental.

**Scene description:** A real plant room: a service machine, a logbook station, a hanging work light on a hook, a coiled hose on a wall bracket. The logbook pages are blank cream rectangles with faint ruled charcoal lines and no lettering of any kind. Every other surface that could carry lettering — the gauge face, the machine panel, the walls, the pen — is blank and unlettered. Open white space in the upper left. Do not fill the frame.

**Context box:** Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: YOU CLEARED THE PATH. SOMEONE ELSE GOT THE CREDIT.
