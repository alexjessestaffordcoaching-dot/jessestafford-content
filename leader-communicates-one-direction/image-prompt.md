## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader is accountable for a result but has no direct line to the peer who actually decides whether it gets done, because he routes every cross-team ask up his own chain instead of talking sideways.
**Context box text:** THE ORG CHART ISN'T HOW THE WORK GETS DONE
**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is organizational-navigation and accountability content. Jesse's expression is clear-eyed and a little dry, the look of someone who learned this the hard way and is now telling you plainly. Concept 3 shifts slightly more wry and self-aware, since he is looking at his own absurd setup.

## Concept 1: The System

**Visual metaphor:** A branching pneumatic-tube or pipe network shaped like an org chart. A request capsule has to travel all the way up one branch, across the top of the frame, and back down another branch to reach the terminal box that sits right next to Jesse's, an arm's length away, because there is no direct pipe between the two same-level boxes.
**First-second read:** Jesse is standing right beside the thing he needs, but the only path connecting them goes way up and all the way over. The detour is absurd, and he is looking at the short empty gap, not the long pipe.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse small and peripheral in the lower-left at his own terminal box. The oversized pipe detour fills the upper two-thirds of the frame. A near-identical peer box sits close enough for him to touch, with a small blank gap and no connector between them.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION (most important):** 16:9 single panel. Jesse stands in the lower-left quadrant, drawn small, at a waist-high labeled terminal box that rises from the floor. Immediately to his right, close enough to reach, sits a second near-identical terminal box (the peer's). Between the two boxes: a narrow blank vertical gap with two unconnected pipe stubs facing each other, no link. Rising out of Jesse's box, an elaborate charcoal-linework pipe network branches upward, runs the full width across the top of the frame, and descends on the far right into the peer box, the long way around. One small capsule is lodged partway up the left branch, not moving. Jesse is turned toward the peer box, one hand raised toward the small gap between them, weight relaxed. The pipe detour is the dominant focal element by scale; Jesse is the human anchor in the corner. Keep the center-right mid-ground as open white space so the detour reads instantly.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision, not sterile flat-art and not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills, visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required; they are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements where objects have exposed mechanics, rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash, never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue, white, and charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed, never stiff or formal. Here: standing at his own terminal box in the lower-left, body angled toward the adjacent peer box, one hand lifted toward the small empty gap between the two boxes, chin slightly down, a direct and faintly dry expression, because he can see how short the real distance is.

**Scene description:** The frame is mostly white. The pipe network is the one busy element and it arcs overhead like plumbing that was routed by a committee. The two terminal boxes at the bottom are plain and boxy, each with a small blank label plate. The stuck capsule on the left branch is the only spot of secondary color. Faint charcoal mechanical texture along the lower edge, exposed gears and routing valves, grounds the boxes without pulling focus. The deliberate emptiness of the gap between the two boxes is the whole point; do not fill it.

**Context box:** Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: THE ORG CHART ISN'T HOW THE WORK GETS DONE

**Tone guardrails:** This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene; it does not compete with it.

## Concept 3: The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Environmental inversion. The org-chart detour is pulled out of the office and into a backyard. Jesse is watering a potted plant three feet from the spigot by running a garden hose all the way around the perimeter of the yard, along the fence and around a tree, before it loops back to the plant beside him. The only thing between the spigot and the plant is an ankle-high garden border he is stepping carefully around instead of over.
**First-second read:** This guy is watering a plant right next to the tap by sending the hose around his entire yard. Why? Because of a fence he could step over without noticing.
**Why it works:** It takes the "route it up the chain" habit somewhere domestic and makes it look exactly as ridiculous as it is, and it shows the "wall" people respect is ankle-high.
