## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader being publicly recognized for something he originally started for entirely selfish reasons, realizing in that moment that honest self-interest directed outward produces better outcomes than performed altruism.

**Context box text:** THE MOTIVATION IS YOURS. THE OUTCOME IS SHARED.

**Emotional tone:** Warm and open. Jesse's expression is a quiet, knowing recognition — not a performance, not surprise, but the calm look of someone who finally has permission to stop explaining himself. He may be smiling slightly. This is permission-to-exhale content, not strategy or accountability content.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A gear train. A small gear (representing the self-interested motivation — ordinary, unremarkable) meshes directly with a much larger gear (representing the team or organizational benefit). The scale difference between the gears is the point: the input is small, the output is large. No hands on the mechanism. It is already in motion.

**First-second read:** A small gear connected to a large gear, both in motion. Jesse observing from the left, slightly peripheral, looking toward the connection point between gears. The viewer reads: something small is driving something much bigger.

**Compositional approach:** Wide shot. The gear mechanism occupies the right two-thirds of the frame — the large gear prominent, the small gear at the point where Jesse's gaze lands. Jesse stands smaller in the lower-left, turned toward the mechanism, looking at the connection point between the gears. His spatial position is observational, not operational. He is not touching or operating the gears. The distance between Jesse and the mechanism reinforces that the output is already happening on its own.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important:
Wide-shot framing, 16:9 ratio. The gear mechanism is the dominant element, occupying the right two-thirds of the frame. The large gear fills most of the right side, rendered at significant scale. The small gear appears at the left edge of the mechanism, where the two gears mesh. Jesse stands smaller in the lower-left portion of the frame, turned toward the right to face the gear train. His scale is roughly one-quarter the height of the large gear. He is not touching the mechanism — his body is angled toward it, his gaze directed at the connection point between the small and large gears. The spatial relationship communicates: the gears are already running without him. He is observing something that has its own momentum.

Art style spec:
Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Color palette:
Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The large gear receives the primary blue (#100299) wash. The small gear is rendered in a warm amber or brass tone, slightly secondary in visual weight, to differentiate it from the large gear without competing. Background mechanical elements in light charcoal wash.

Expression: calm recognition — not surprise, not pride, but the quiet look of someone understanding how something works. Not quite smiling, but settled.

A precision gear mechanism dominates the right two-thirds of the frame. The large gear is prominent — slightly tilted, rendered with exposed mechanical detail in the Schematic Wash style: visible teeth, internal spokes, the weight of a real industrial component. At the left edge of the large gear, a small gear meshes with it — clearly smaller, clearly ordinary. Both gears are in motion, suggested by faint motion lines on the teeth. The background steampunk mechanical environment reinforces the systems theme without competing — pipes, valves, and clock-gear elements rendered as light charcoal linework texture. A single directional shadow from the large gear falls toward the left, toward Jesse. Negative space across the upper-left of the frame is intentional.

## Concept 2 — The Room

**Scene dynamic:** Jesse and one other person in a genuine peer-level exchange at a simple surface — a coffee table or a bench. The other person is slightly younger. Neither person is lecturing or presenting. They are leaning toward each other in the posture of two people genuinely invested in the same conversation. This is the outcome the post is about: the real connection that followed from a self-interested start.

**First-second read:** Two people at equal height, facing each other, leaning in. The arrangement immediately reads as genuine mutual investment — peer-level, not hierarchical. No gap, no distance, no observer-and-observed.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse on the left side of the frame in his blue shirt. The other person on the right in neutral charcoal tones, body slightly angled toward Jesse. Both at the same vertical height in the frame — deliberately even, not one higher or more prominent than the other. The surface between them (a small table or bench) carries a coffee cup or notebook — something that grounds the scene in a real conversation rather than a meeting. Background mechanical detail is light and recedes.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale inversion. A tiny, almost toy-like wind-up key mechanism — representing the unremarkable self-interested origin (I wanted better interviews) — is connected via an oversized mechanical shaft to a massive industrial gear wheel that dominates the right side of the frame. The incongruity between the small, ordinary mechanism and the enormous output it drives is the point. Jesse stands beside the small mechanism, not the large gear, with a slight knowing smile.

**First-second read:** An enormous gear wheel and a tiny wind-up mechanism connected by a shaft. The viewer's eye immediately catches the absurd scale difference. Then notices Jesse standing beside the small mechanism — not the large one. The realization: Jesse is standing next to the "ordinary" origin, and what it produced fills the rest of the frame.

**Why it works:** The selfish motivation was small and mundane (I was frustrated by bad interviews). What it produced — years of recognized impact on students — was disproportionately large. The scale inversion makes this concrete and ironic in a way the post's text can only describe. Standing beside the small mechanism, rather than the large gear, locates Jesse at the origin rather than the outcome.
