I have given feedback I did not actually want to give.

It showed in the room, not in what I said. The words were fine. The investment was not there, and neither of us walked away thinking something had changed.

The feedback that has worked on my teams has mostly come from a self-interested place. I wanted the specific behavior to stop because it was costing me. Not the team. Me. That is not the version management training teaches. But a reason you actually believe gets you to a more direct conversation than one you borrowed.

The self-interest does not undermine the feedback. It is what keeps you from softening the message until it means nothing.

What feedback are you holding back because your reason did not feel good enough?
