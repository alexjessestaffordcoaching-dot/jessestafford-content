## Judgment Calls Made

**1. Citation phrasing simplified throughout**
The research paragraph originally cited "Dr. Paul McCarthy, who researches cognitive reframing in competitive sports" and "Zhu et al., Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology." Both were simplified to sound like Jesse at a bar: "It turns out sports psychologists have been studying this for years" and "A 2025 workplace study found the same pattern." The full citations are preserved only in the Blogger post (where the sourced fact spec applies), with working links. Medium intentionally uses the conversational version.

**2. Learner/Judger labels: first example keeps them, second example drops them**
The deadline example ("Judger question: 'What is wrong with this team?' Learner question: '...'") keeps explicit labels because it introduces the Marilee Adams framework for the first time. The promotion example ("Why does this always happen to me?" vs. "What do the people who made this decision not know about my work?") drops the labels and runs conversationally — the concept is already established by that point. This was Jordan's suggestion; Sam agreed it removed structural repetition.

**3. Sports opening: built from Story 71 exact text, not reconstructed**
The opening ("I was in the middle of a game. My body hurt. My heart was pounding...") is directly derived from Story 71 source language. Some physical detail (slowing breathing deliberately, pushing past the limit) is a natural expansion of "I learned that I could mentally overcome pain, slow down my heart, to keep pushing harder." This is a composite of implication from the source, not invention. Jesse should confirm this matches how he actually remembers the experience.

**4. Title: chose from research candidates**
Morgan offered five title candidates. "How to Stay Clear-Headed When Everything at Work Is Falling Apart" was selected as the strongest for Medium — specific, high-stakes, searchable, and doesn't use the word "mindset" (avoiding the overuse flag Morgan noted).
