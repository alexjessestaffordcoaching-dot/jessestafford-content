## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader has been adding urgency to a team that is already moving in the right direction — the realization is that the pushing itself is the friction, not the fuel.

**Context box text:** CHECK THE DIRECTION BEFORE YOU ADD URGENCY

**Emotional tone:** Calm and grounded. Jesse's expression is deliberate and unhurried — the look of someone who has learned to ask the question before reacting. Not worried, not urgent. Clear-eyed and composed.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A rowboat in schematic side-view — oars resting in their locks, unused. The boat is already in motion (schematic wake lines trail behind the stern). Jesse stands at the tiller, checking direction, not pulling oars.

**First-second read:** A boat moving forward with the oars at rest — the momentum is already there. The person at the helm is checking which way it's pointed, not rowing harder.

**Compositional approach:** The boat fills the full horizontal span of the frame. Jesse stands at the stern (right side), slightly smaller in scale than the boat mechanism — he is in the direction position, not the power position. Open negative space in front of the bow shows the path is clear. The resting oars and the trailing wake are the two details that tell the story before the context box is read.

**First-second read:** Two people across from each other, one of whom has just gone still — the pause after something landed.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse on the left, slightly reclined and open in his seat. The manager on the right, leaning slightly forward — he came in anxious, and the posture shift is mid-resolve. Equal scale — coaching relationship, not hierarchical. Visible space between them in the center of the frame — the quiet lives there.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale contrast. A large organizational system fills the background — gears, flow arrows, pipe networks — and it is visibly in motion. Jesse stands in the lower-left of the frame, small relative to the system, holding a compass and looking at it. His hands are not on the system. He is not pushing it. The system is already running.

**First-second read:** A man with a compass standing in front of a large running system he is not touching. The system doesn't need him to push it.

**Why it works:** The conventional leadership image would show Jesse engaging with or driving the system. This image shows him stepping back from it and checking a single small tool. The reframe is in scale and position — the most purposeful thing in the frame is the smallest one. The compass, not the system.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important:
Wide shot, 16:9 aspect ratio. A large schematic organizational system fills the upper-right two-thirds of the frame — an interconnected network of gears, flow arrows, pipe sections, and process nodes. The system is rendered with motion indicators: schematic rotation arrows on gears, directional arrows on flow paths, velocity lines on pipes. It is clearly running without any human intervention. Jesse stands in the lower-left of the frame, noticeably smaller in scale relative to the system behind him. He faces slightly right, toward the system, but his attention is on the compass in both hands — a small, precise instrument. He is not touching any element of the system. His body language is deliberate and still. The visual tension is in the contrast between the running system's scale and Jesse's deliberate stillness with the small navigation tool. The center of the frame, between Jesse and the system, is open — the gap between leader and machine is intentional.
