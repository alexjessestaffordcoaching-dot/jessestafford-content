## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A manager is about to hand someone a category label like "communicate better" instead of a specific, actionable instruction, and in the moment before speaking, realizes it won't land.
**Context box text:** VAGUE FEEDBACK IS A CATEGORY, NOT AN INSTRUCTION
**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is a clarity-and-specificity topic, not a warmth topic. Jesse's expression should read as present and deliberate, a person catching himself before defaulting to the easy, vague version.

---

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A control panel with four labeled, wired-in dials (SITUATION, BEHAVIOR, IMPACT, BETTER), each reading a specific, calibrated value. Off to the side, a single unlabeled switch hangs loose, disconnected from the panel, wired to nothing.
**First-second read:** A precise four-dial instrument panel is doing real work. One isolated switch nearby is not wired into anything and does nothing. The viewer understands: specific inputs run the system, and the vague one is not even connected to it.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse close and deliberate at the panel, hand resting near the four active dials, environment organized around his position (the "clarity/decision" spatial rule). The disconnected switch sits smaller and peripheral, off to his left, clearly outside the working system.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide shot, 16:9. A four-dial instrument control panel fills the center-right two-thirds of the frame, each dial clearly calibrated with a small engraved label beneath it reading SITUATION, BEHAVIOR, IMPACT, and BETTER, needles pointing to specific, non-zero values, visible wiring running from each dial into a central housing. Jesse stands close to the panel, right of center, one hand resting near the dials, weight settled, looking at the panel with focus. In the lower-left of the frame, smaller and set apart, a single unlabeled toggle switch hangs from a short length of disconnected wire, not attached to the panel housing, doing nothing. Negative space surrounds the disconnected switch so it reads as isolated and inert against the active, wired panel.

Art style spec: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision, not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills, visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required, these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements, objects have exposed mechanics, rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Color palette: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash, never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. The four active dials can carry a small warm accent tone to read as "live," while the disconnected switch stays charcoal and white only, reinforcing that it is inert.

Primary character: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed, never stiff or formal. Standing close to the panel, one hand resting near the dials, shoulders settled, expression focused and present, a person paying close attention rather than performing for the camera.

Scene description: The four-dial panel is the clear focal element, wired, calibrated, and visibly functioning. The disconnected switch is a small, deliberate secondary detail, positioned with enough negative space around it that it cannot be mistaken for part of the working system. Background mechanical linework (gears, pipe runs, small exposed housings) is sparse and abstract, reinforcing the idea of a larger system without competing with the panel. Do not fill the frame; leave open white space around both the panel and the disconnected switch.

Context box: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: VAGUE FEEDBACK IS A CATEGORY, NOT AN INSTRUCTION

Tone guardrails: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene, it does not compete with it.

## Concept 2 — The Room

**Scene dynamic:** Jesse sits across a small table from a direct report during a 1-on-1. Jesse pauses before speaking, looking down at his own notepad, on which only a single word is written. The direct report leans forward slightly, waiting for something concrete that has not arrived yet.
**First-second read:** A manager is caught mid-pause, looking at a notepad with just one word on it, while the person across from him waits expectantly. The viewer reads the hesitation as recognition: he knows one word is not enough.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse and the direct report at roughly equal scale, facing each other across a small table (peer dynamic, not hierarchy), but Jesse's gaze is down and inward on the notepad rather than meeting the other person's eyes, communicating the moment of catching himself before speaking.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale inversion. An oversized, generic sticky note reading "COMMUNICATE BETTER" is plastered directly over a detailed instrument panel, completely obscuring the actual dials underneath. Jesse is mid-motion, peeling back one corner of the giant note to reveal the specific, calibrated dials hidden beneath it.
**First-second read:** A huge, generic label is covering up something detailed and specific, and someone is in the middle of pulling it back to reveal what was underneath the whole time. The viewer gets it immediately: the vague label was never the real information, it was just sitting on top of it.
**Why it works:** It makes the post's central claim physical and immediate. The reader doesn't have to infer that vague feedback hides something more useful, they see it literally being peeled back in real time.

Jesse stands just right of center, one hand mid-motion peeling back the note's lower corner, revealing a glimpse of two calibrated dials underneath with small labels partially visible. Jesse's body is angled toward the panel, not toward the viewer, weight forward, actively mid-action. Leave open white space above the panel so the scale of the oversized note reads clearly against the smaller, precise dials beneath it.
