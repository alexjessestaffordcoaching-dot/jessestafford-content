## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A manager is about to hand someone a category label like "communicate better" or "be more strategic" instead of a specific behavior, and the fix — naming the situation, the behavior, the impact, and what better looks like — takes the same ten seconds once you make yourself specific in advance.

**Context box text:** A CATEGORY ISN'T FEEDBACK. NAME THE BEHAVIOR.

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is a clarity-and-specificity topic, not a warmth topic. Jesse's expression reads present and deliberate — a person catching himself a beat before defaulting to the easy, vague version. (Concept 3 — The Reframe carries the same tone with a faint wry, caught set to the mouth: he has just noticed what he was about to hand over.)

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A stack of printed performance-review forms on Jesse's desk at the end of review season. The top form has exactly one line filled in — a two-word category — and every other ruled field on the page is blank. The stack is the physical residue of years of feedback that never said anything specific. It fits because this post's story is literally about the sentence a manager writes and the sentences they leave blank.

**Setting:** Jesse's office desk, end of the review cycle.

**First-second read:** A whole stack of review forms, and the only thing written on the top one is a two-word label.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse close and deliberate at the desk (clarity/decision positioning), one hand flat on the stack, head tipped down toward that single filled-in line. The desk instruments are organized around his position. Negative space held in the upper-left wall plane.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** The absent element, made literal. Jesse is handing a colleague a plain cardboard box across a desk — and the box is open toward the viewer and empty, except for one small slip at the bottom with a single word on it. This is "a category label with nothing inside it," rendered as an object you could actually pick up. Jesse is looking down into the box rather than at the colleague — the beat where he notices he is about to hand over nothing.

**Setting:** An office, across a desk — a place where one person hands another person something.

**First-second read:** He is giving someone a box, and there is nothing in it.

**Why it works:** The post's central claim — vague feedback only looks like feedback — stops being something the reader has to infer. The empty box is right there, mid-handoff.
