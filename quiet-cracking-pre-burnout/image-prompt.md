## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A manager looks at output data that says everything is fine — but the signal that matters is in a 1-on-1 conversation, not on any dashboard. The high performer is still delivering. They are also quietly cracking.

**Context box text:** THE OUTPUT LOOKS FINE. ASK THE QUESTION ANYWAY.

**Emotional tone:** Warm and open. Jesse is attentive and present, not performing warmth. In Concepts 1 and 2, his expression is quiet and focused — the look of someone who knows to listen rather than read the room from a distance. In Concept 3, expression shifts: calm and studious, absorbed in data, unaware of what is behind him.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A monitoring station with multiple readout panels — all indicators showing nominal, green, fine. Jesse stands small and apart on the left, not looking at the screens. He is angled toward something off-frame: a conversation the data cannot show him.

**First-second read:** The readout says everything is okay. But the person in the frame is not looking at the readout.

**Compositional approach:** The monitoring panel and screens occupy the right two-thirds of the frame, tall and prominent. Jesse is in the lower left — small relative to the system, body turned slightly away from the data, facing the viewer or the left edge, as if listening to someone just outside the frame. He is not touching the panel. The gap between Jesse and the screens is the concept.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important:
Wide establishing shot. The monitoring panel dominates the right two-thirds of the frame — tall screens filled with schematic readouts, gauges, and indicators, all showing nominal green readings in charcoal linework. Jesse stands in the lower-left quadrant, occupying no more than one-fifth of the total frame width. He is not touching or looking at the panel. His body is angled left, slightly away from the data — posture of quiet attention toward something just outside the left edge of the frame, as if someone just spoke and he is listening. The gap between Jesse and the system is visible and deliberate. The panel reads: fine. Jesse reads: still listening.

Art style spec:
Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Color palette:
Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. Screens and readout elements in cool muted tones (faint sage or grey-green watercolor) to suggest "nominal" without competing with the primary blue.

Primary character:
Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Standing still, body angled left, weight slightly forward — posture of someone listening rather than watching. Expression: calm and attentive, slightly tilted head, as if something just caught his attention in the space the data cannot measure.

Scene description:
Right two-thirds of the frame: a tall multi-screen monitoring station rendered in careful schematic linework. Each screen shows readout indicators — lines, gauges, status lights — all in the normal range. The screens are the most detailed element in the frame. They are also, visibly, not what Jesse is paying attention to. Left one-fifth of the frame: Jesse, small relative to the system, standing at a slight diagonal away from the panel. He is looking toward the left edge, toward something the frame does not show. The space between Jesse and the panel is open negative space — white, deliberate. Background mechanical elements (exposed gearing, pipe systems, schematic line art) are faint and secondary, suggesting the organizational context in which this moment lives.

Context box:
Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the bottom of the panel. Text: THE OUTPUT LOOKS FINE. ASK THE QUESTION ANYWAY.

Tone guardrails:
This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The monitoring panel is large because the data is what everyone trusts. Jesse is small and turned away because the conversation is where the real signal is. The mechanical background supports the scene — it does not compete with it.

## Concept 2 — The Room

**Scene dynamic:** Jesse sits across from one other person at a small table. The other person is in mid-pause — leaning slightly forward, looking down at their hands on the table. They are deciding whether to answer honestly. Jesse is open, still, slightly forward. Two mugs on the table. The question has just been asked. The pause is the concept.

**First-second read:** Two people at a table. One is pausing before answering. The viewer reads the weight of the pause — something real just got asked.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse and the other person are at equal scale, sitting across from each other at a small table. The other person's downward gaze and hands on the table anchor the frame's emotional center. Jesse is slightly forward on his side — present, not crowding. The table is between them, small enough that the distance is intimate. No third parties. Background is minimal: a sparse room with faint mechanical environmental detail.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Environmental inversion. Jesse sits at a simple table reviewing a printout or screen showing all-green performance metrics — absorbed in the data, expression calm and studious. Behind him, in the background, a wall has a visible hairline crack running through its plaster. He cannot see it from his angle. The crack is visible. The data is not.

**First-second read:** A man reading data that says everything is fine while a crack appears in the structure behind him. The viewer gets it immediately: the signal is somewhere the data cannot show.

**Why it works:** The domestic setting (a table, a wall, a crack) makes the concept land through irony. The performance readout is prominent and green. The crack in the wall is quiet but visible. The image does the work the text does — the output looks fine; what's actually happening is somewhere else.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important:
Medium shot. Jesse sits at a simple table in the center-left of the frame, facing slightly toward the viewer, looking down at a printed report or open laptop. The screen or printout faces toward the viewer — readable enough to suggest rows of green indicators, nominal readings, all-clear metrics. Jesse's expression is calm and focused on the data. In the background behind and above Jesse, the wall of the room has a hairline crack running diagonally — visible, specific, unmistakable, but not dramatic. Jesse cannot see it from his seated angle. The crack occupies the upper-right background of the frame. The proportions are important: Jesse and his data are in the foreground, normal-sized and detailed; the crack is behind him, smaller in the frame but impossible to miss once you see it.

Art style spec:
Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. The wall's crack is rendered in clean linework, specific and precise. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Color palette:
Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The performance readout screen uses faint cool green watercolor for its indicators — only enough to read as "nominal." The crack in the wall gets no special color treatment — it is charcoal linework on the white wall, making it quiet but precise. A warm mug in amber on the table adds a domestic grounding element.

Primary character:
Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Seated, slightly forward, looking down at the report or screen in front of him. Expression: calm, focused, reading — fully absorbed in the data. He does not know there is a crack behind him. His posture is relaxed and attentive — a person who trusts what the readout is showing him.

Note on expression: This concept depicts the manager before the question is asked — reading the data, not looking up. Expression is intentionally different from Concepts 1 and 2: studious and absorbed, not attentive and listening.

Scene description:
A simple table — a mug, a laptop or printed report. Jesse seated, looking at the screen or printout. The report shows schematic rows of data, all indicators in the normal range (rendered in light cool green watercolor line art). Behind Jesse, the wall of a plain room is visible. Running diagonally through the wall, starting near the upper right corner of the background and traveling partway down and left: a single hairline crack in the plaster, rendered in careful charcoal linework. The crack is not dramatic. It is not large. It is specific and visible. The environment is spare — a suggestion of an office or home office, not a fully rendered room. Background mechanical elements are faint at the periphery, reinforcing the idea of systems around this ordinary moment.

Context box:
Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the bottom of the panel. Text: THE OUTPUT LOOKS FINE. ASK THE QUESTION ANYWAY.

This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific.
