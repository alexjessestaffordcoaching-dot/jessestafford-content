## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A director tells the room AI wrote his planning memo in an hour, everyone smiles, and then he asks whether the team can pull the delivery date in, while the manager holds two true pictures and has to answer.
**Context box text:** BOTH PICTURES ARE TRUE. SAY YES FIRST.
**Emotional tone:** Calm and grounded. The manager is composed in a room that is smiling; his expression is steady and attentive, mouth closed, eyes on the person he is about to answer, with a slight pause in his face.
**Note on the scene:** the director and the room are a Jesse-approved composite. No real identifiable person or company is depicted, and no frame contains lettering other than the context box where one is specified. No em dashes in any image text.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** The wall planner a team keeps for its delivery date: a rail with a sliding date peg, work blocks in two lanes, and the gates and stamp press that approvals physically are. Every "pull the date in" request ends up on a board like this one, and the board shows what a memo cannot: one lane is easy to move and one lane is behind gates.
**Setting:** A team room with one long wall planner, a table and a window.
**First-second read:** Someone is moving the deadline earlier, and half the work is behind closed gates.
**Compositional approach:** The manager small and slightly apart from the planner, looking at the gate lane, while the director's hand is on the peg. Scale and distance carry the idea; he is not operating anything.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important**
Wide 16:9 interior of a team room. The wall planner fills the right two-thirds of the frame; its delivery-date peg sits at the far right end of the rail. A man in a charcoal jacket (the director), at equal height, stands at the far right edge with one hand on the peg, mostly in profile and turned away. The man in the blue shirt stands in the lower-left third, noticeably smaller than the planner (about a third of its height), three steps back from it, looking toward the gate lane. He is peripheral to the planner but clearly the only person looking at the lower lane. Context box at the extreme bottom.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important**
Objects and working mechanisms: a wall planner with a horizontal rail and a rack-and-pinion delivery-date peg with a brass thumbscrew, its top lane of work blocks sliding freely in grooves and its lower lane sitting behind two pivoting barrier gates with visible counterweights; a lever-operated stamp press with a spring return and a gear sector, holding a stack of blank forms; a window with louvered blinds and a visible tilt rod and gearbox; swivel chairs with visible spring-tilt mechanisms. The charcoal schematic layer sits unpainted in the upper-left wall plane above the window and in the upper margin: blueprint-style linework of a gear train, a linkage and dimension lines, with no lettering.

## Carousel

**Shared setting:** a mid-size team room with one long wall holding a wall-mounted release planner, a long table, and a tall window with louvered blinds.
**Shared mechanism family:** barrier gates, lever stamp presses with spring returns, pivot arms and counterweights, rack-and-pinion pegs, louvered-blind linkages.

**Deck read-through:**
1. AI saved him an hour. — Your team still has the same week. That gap is where the update gets awkward.
2. Then he asked to pull the date in. — The room was smiling. I had two answers ready and neither fit: "Sure" was not true, and "it does not quite work that way" sounded like pushback.
3. I tried the direct answer first. — I said it was not working out, that we were waiting on decisions and review. His smile went flat, and he asked if the team was even using the tools.
4. Both of them were telling the truth. — His work is drafting and summarizing, where AI is quick. My team's work runs through decisions and approvals from other people. Different work, both real.
5. Step one: say yes to what is true. — Agree with the part of the story that is real. "Yes, the drafting is faster, and my team feels it."
6. Steps two and three: where the time goes. — Say where the rest of the time goes. Then give the date you can stand behind, or the date you will know.
7. Write your two sentences before the meeting. — What is the sentence you wish you had said the last time someone told you AI was making everyone faster?

**Cold-read check:** (a) Yes. By slide 2 I know the problem: a leader praised AI, asked to pull a date in, and the manager had no good answer ready. (b) Yes. Slide 3 tells the story on its own (the direct answer made the room hear pushback) and slide 4 gives the turn in one idea (both were telling the truth because the work is different). (c) Yes. By slide 6 I have a specific thing to do: say yes to what is true, say where the rest of the time goes, and give the date I can stand behind or the date I will know; slide 7 says to write those two sentences before the meeting. (d) Yes. Each slide follows from the one before it and none depends on the picture to make sense.
