An engineer on a game team could see the release date was going to slip. They went to the project manager and said it straight: "You need to move the date by two weeks. We are late."

That conversation did not go the way the engineer expected. The PM pushed back and started asking questions, and the engineer walked out feeling like their honest read of the situation had just been waved off.

I have watched this play out on team after team. The engineer was right about the facts, and it still went sideways, because a flat directive from an engineer to a PM lands like a power play instead of a heads-up.

Picture the same moment with a different sentence: "Things are slipping and I need two more weeks to get back on track." Same facts, but now it is a request from someone who owns the problem, and the PM has something to work with. A better frame will not guarantee you get the two weeks, but it keeps the door open instead of turning it into a standoff.

The technical work is usually fine. What trips people up is that being right and being heard are two different skills, and most engineers only ever got coached on the first one.

Where are you being completely right in a way that almost guarantees you get dismissed?
