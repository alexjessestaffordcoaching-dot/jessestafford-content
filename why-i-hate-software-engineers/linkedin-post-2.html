I have sat in rooms where the first thirty minutes went to debating what "done" means for a feature nobody had started building.

The never ending discussions about semantics are on my list of the things I find hardest about managing engineers. The instinct behind them is a good one, though. Engineers get rewarded for precision, and catching two people who use one word two ways is the same skill that makes the code work.

A planning meeting is there to reach a decision, and in that room the precision is valuable while the context is the problem.

You can give the room that context by opening with, "Before we define anything, here is what we have to decide today, and by when." It will not end every argument about words, but it tells people how exact a definition needs to be for that decision.

When your team gets stuck on a word, what decision is the room actually supposed to be making?

If you want a thinking partner for that kind of conversation, I am around.
