## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader is about to label a strong performer a "bad communicator," when the real problem is that the leader has been delivering information the same way to everyone, and this one person needed a different channel.
**Context box text:** STOP MISTAKING COMMUNICATION STYLE FOR A PERFORMANCE PROBLEM
**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is a systems-and-decision post, not a warmth-and-connection one. Jesse's expression should read as alert and deliberate, mid-realization, not smiling and not stressed.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** An old brass multi-channel radio receiver with several frequency dials. Most dials sit dark and mismatched. One dial is lit and aligned, glowing faintly where the needle sits exactly on its marked frequency line.
**First-second read:** A person is carefully adjusting one dial on a bank of old radio receivers, several of which are dark and out of alignment, one of which glows because it is correctly tuned. The viewer understands, before any text, that getting the right signal through is about finding the right frequency, not about the signal being weak.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse is positioned close and deliberate, slightly left of center, one dial at hand height directly in front of him. The receiver bank fills the right two-thirds of the frame at a scale slightly larger than Jesse, so the mechanism reads as the subject and Jesse reads as the person actively working it, not dwarfed by it.

His body is turned three-quarters toward the mechanism, not facing the viewer directly. Generous white negative space above and to the left of him.

Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash, never solid black fill. The one lit dial carries a warm amber-gold watercolor glow as a supporting color, it must not compete with the blue, white, and charcoal triad.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed, weight settled on one hip, reaching toward the dial with one hand, not touching it yet. Expression alert and focused, mid-realization, brow slightly drawn, not smiling and not tense.

The one glowing dial's needle sits precisely on its printed line, radiating a soft amber wash that is the only warm color note in an otherwise blue-white-charcoal scene. Jesse's free hand hangs relaxed at his side. Behind him, the white paper canvas is mostly open, with faint charcoal gear outlines suggesting a larger mechanical system extending off-frame.

TONE GUARDRAILS: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene, it does not compete with it.

The engineer sits with low-key, closed-off posture, arms loosely crossed, notebook on the table rather than a laptop open. Jesse has matched the register down rather than leaning in with big energy.
**First-second read:** Two people sitting at a low-key, quiet distance apart in what reads as a calm one-on-one, neither one performing for the other. The viewer reads restraint and attentiveness before anything else, not tension and not warmth-heavy connection.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse and the other person are seated at roughly equal scale, at a slight angle to each other rather than face-to-face across a table, so the arrangement reads as alongside rather than confrontational or hierarchical. Negative space separates them slightly more than a typical friendly conversation would, reinforcing that Jesse is deliberately giving this person room rather than pressing in.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale inversion. A performance review page, blown up to an enormous, oversized scale, dominates the frame with the handwritten phrase "COMMUNICATION ISSUES" circled in red, while Jesse stands small at the bottom edge holding a pen just above the page, paused before writing the final word.
**First-second read:** An oversized document towers over a small figure holding a pen just short of the page, mid-hesitation. The viewer immediately reads that whatever is about to be written down carries far more weight than the moment itself suggests.
**Why it works:** It makes the cost of a lazy label visible and physical. The post argues that a vague "communication issues" note in a review does real, lasting damage, and rendering that phrase as an oversized, looming object while Jesse hesitates at a tiny scale makes that consequence impossible to miss in a single glance.
