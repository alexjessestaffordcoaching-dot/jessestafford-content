## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** Someone read the whole contract, caught what the AI summary got wrong, and nothing in the room shows that it happened.

**Context box text:** A GOOD CATCH LOOKS LIKE A QUIET WEEK

**Emotional tone:** Calm and grounded. This is a quiet-composure, unrewarded-diligence topic, not a warm-connection or strategy topic. Jesse's expression across concepts is steady and a little rueful, someone who did the careful thing and knows nobody will bring it up.

## Concept 1 - The Evidence

**The artifact:** A thick, heavily flagged contract sitting next to a thin, clean one-page summary. The stack is the only trace of the checking, and no one else will open it.
**Setting:** A plain conference room table in an ordinary office.
**First-second read:** A man alone at a table with a thick marked-up stack, while a single clean sheet sits untouched across from him. Careful work with nobody to see it.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse close and deliberate at the near end, environment organized around his position, the two documents carrying the contrast through presence of mass (thick versus one sheet), not subtle size.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION - most important:** 16:9 single panel. A plain conference room table seen from a slightly raised three-quarter angle, running from lower-left to center-right. Jesse sits at the near end in the lower-left third, small-to-medium at human scale, one forearm on the table, looking down at the table's center. A thick bound contract, roughly four inches deep, lies open in front of him, its page edges crowded with blank colored sticky flags. At the far end of the table, alone, lies a single crisp one-page summary sheet, centered in the right third, square to the table edge and untouched. The thick marked stack and the thin clean sheet are unmistakably different in mass. The empty chairs around the table are pushed in. Upper-right is open wall and window, mostly white negative space.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT - second most important:** Objects and their working mechanism: a heavy-duty desktop stapler on the table with its hinged arm, spring, and anvil plate exposed in cutaway; a three-ring binder lying open with its ring mechanism visible, the rings parted along their hinge; an articulated desk lamp on the table's far corner with its pantograph arms and spring tensioners drawn visibly. Charcoal schematic linework occupies the upper-right wall plane above the table and the upper-left corner: blueprint-style annotation of the stapler's lever and spring and of the binder-ring clamp. The S mark is worked into the baseplate of the stapler as a cast emblem, rendered in charcoal.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision , not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required , these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash, never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. Supporting warm ochre for the table wood and a soft muted red for the sticky flags, kept small.

Relaxed, unposed, never stiff or formal. ## Carousel

**Shared setting:** An ordinary mid-size office: a glass-walled meeting room, the hallway outside it, the manager's office, and Jesse's desk.
**Shared mechanism family:** Paper and desk hardware with visible working mechanism: staplers, binder rings, retractable pen cams, laptop hinges, articulated desk lamps, swivel-chair gas lifts.

**Deck read-through:**

1. A good catch looks like a quiet week. - I read a whole contract because I had some moments. A teammate had an AI summary of it instead.

2. The summary had errors nobody could see. - It had hallucinations and errors that would have set back our negotiations. Because I read the source, they were not set back. Nobody reacted.

3. Handled well, the work stays invisible. - Our bug badges before release turned up problems that would have been major after launch. We managed them early, and almost nobody noticed.

4. Managers only know what you tell them. - They amplify that message to the people above them. People tend to believe their manager knows everything they do, and it just is not true.

5. 1. Make it about business impact. - Not technical challenge. Give your manager something they can repeat with minimal translation. If they have to decode it first, it stops with them.

6. 2. Name the catch, the cost, the fix. - One line, sent the same day: what you caught, what it would have cost the business, what you did. Write it before you close the ticket.

7. Your manager cannot repeat what they never heard. - If you caught something this week before anyone else saw it, who knows?


**Cold-read check:** (a) Yes: slide 1 shows someone reading a contract that a teammate summarized with AI, and slide 2 says the summary had errors that were caught and nobody reacted. (b) Yes: slide 3 gives the turn in plain words (problems caught before release, almost nobody noticed) and slide 4 states the insight (managers only know what you tell them), and neither needs the post. (c) Yes: slide 6 names the move, one line sent the same day saying what you caught, what it would have cost, and what you did, written before closing the ticket. (d) Yes: each slide follows from the last, problem to cost to why it stays invisible to why the manager does not know to the two-step fix to the close, and no slide depends on the picture to make sense.
