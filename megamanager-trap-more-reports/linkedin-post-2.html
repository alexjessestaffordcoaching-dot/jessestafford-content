I had twenty-eight direct reports once. Most of the few people I gave extra time to are managers and directors now.

Everyone still got a 1-on-1, thirty minutes a month at that size. A handful of people each kept track of an area for me, and I met with them more often. That felt a little unfair to everyone else, and I knew it.

In one of those syncs I handed someone a decision that used to be mine, which project got our people first, and listened while they made it. I was glad to share the load, and a little uncomfortable watching it go differently than I would have done it.

At the time it felt like I wasn't doing my job. Looking back, it was the practice they needed. I'd hand those decisions over sooner now, and I guess something worked out.

If you're stretched thin and handing decisions to the few people you lean on feels like you aren't doing your job, that's where I support leaders through coaching.
