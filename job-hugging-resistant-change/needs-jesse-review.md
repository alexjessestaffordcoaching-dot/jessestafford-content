## Story Used
**Story 70 — The QA Team That Grieved the Goal Change.** Riley identified this as the closest match in the story bank: Jesse changed how a long-tenured QA team's success was measured (from individual defect counts to whole-team, customer-facing outcomes), the team called it unfair, and Jesse's real insight was that this wasn't resistance in the abstract, it was grief over losing a comfortable, identity-defining way of working. No exact 1:1 story existed for "an individual long-tenured employee resisting a new process while their job isn't actually at risk," so this team-level story was adapted to speak to the individual framing this post needed, without inventing any dialogue or details beyond what's in the source text.

Exact source text used as the anchor:
> "Most recently I saw it in a QA team that was incorporated into my team. They were used to having individual goals about their own work, with concessions about what stayed in their control. Lots of complaints about it not being fair and telling me that there is nothing they can to to make sure the goals worked... I changed the goals to be about how many defects were reported by customers, and if the entire game team delivered on time. This means the team has to change how they work with the rest of the game team."
