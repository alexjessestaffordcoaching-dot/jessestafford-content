A tenured employee pushes back on a new process. The instinct is to read it as an attitude problem and respond with pressure.

That instinct is aimed at the wrong target. The person is protecting the one thing that has made them good at the job for years: the specific way they have always done it.

I saw this on a QA team I inherited. I changed how success was measured, nobody's job moved, and they still called it unfair. It took me longer than it should have to hear that what I was calling resistance was actually grief.

Pressure teaches someone to hide the fear better. The first thing that actually helps is saying plainly that their job is not at risk, before you say anything about what needs to change. From there: explain why the role is genuinely shifting, help them build the new skills, and find something in the change they can actually get excited about.

What have you been reading as resistance on your team that might actually be someone grieving a version of the job they used to be great at?
