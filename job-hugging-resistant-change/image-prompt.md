## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A manager realizes a long-tenured employee's pushback against a new process isn't resistance, it's grief over losing the identity of being the expert, and the fix is naming that their job is safe before asking anything else of them.

**Context box text:** SAY THE JOB IS SAFE BEFORE YOU SAY ANYTHING ELSE

**Emotional tone:** Calm and grounded. This is a difficult-conversation, composure-under-pressure topic — Jesse's expression should read as steady and direct, not warm/smiling and not clinical. The face of someone who has had this conversation before and knows how to hold it.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A steampunk apparatus with two distinct parts: a heavy, riveted foundation plinth bolted directly into the floor (unmoving, permanent) supporting an upper gear assembly where one worn brass gear is mid-swap for a newer one. The bolted foundation represents the job itself, stable and untouched. The gear assembly above represents the method, which is what's actually changing.
**First-second read:** The base is clearly anchored and immovable; the change is confined to the mechanism sitting on top of it. The viewer reads "the foundation isn't what's moving here."
**Compositional approach:** Jesse stands at lower-left, smaller in scale, one hand resting flat on the riveted foundation plate. The gear assembly fills the right two-thirds of the frame, rising above him, with one old gear held out mid-removal by an exposed mechanical arm and a new gear waiting alongside it.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide shot, 16:9. A riveted, bolted foundation plinth occupies the lower-left third of the frame, clearly fixed into the floor with visible bolt heads and rivets. Jesse stands at the foundation, smaller in scale relative to the machine, one hand resting flat and still on the plinth's surface, body turned to look upward. Rising from the plinth and filling the right two-thirds of the frame is a tall exposed gear assembly, patent-drawing precision, with one worn brass gear held out mid-removal by a simple mechanical arm, and a newer gear of the same size waiting just beside it, not yet installed. The foundation and the gear assembly are visually distinct: the base is heavy, still, and anchored; the gear assembly above is mid-motion. Generous white negative space surrounds both.

Art style spec: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

Color palette: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. The old gear can carry a worn brass/amber supporting tone; the new gear stays closer to the charcoal/white palette to read as "not yet claimed."

Primary character: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Standing at the foundation plinth, one hand resting flat on the bolted base, weight settled and still, looking upward at the gear assembly with a calm, steady, grounded expression — someone confirming the base is solid before anything above it moves.

Scene description: The riveted foundation plinth is the visual anchor of the frame, its bolts and seams drawn with patent-drawing clarity. The gear assembly rising from it shows one gear caught mid-transfer, exposed mechanical arms and linework suggesting the swap is careful and deliberate, not chaotic. No other figures in the frame. Background steampunk linework (faint pipe runs, small secondary gears) stays abstract and recedes behind the two main elements. Negative space above and to the left of Jesse keeps the frame from feeling crowded.

Context box: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: SAY THE JOB IS SAFE BEFORE YOU SAY ANYTHING ELSE

Tone guardrails: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene — it does not compete with it.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** The absent element, paired with scale contrast. In the foreground, small and precisely rendered, sits a worn brass service plaque (something like a long-service marker) resting untouched on its own small shelf. Behind it, at a much larger scale, a full mechanical retrofit is underway — old machinery being swapped for new. The expected move would be for the retrofit to sweep the plaque away too. Instead, the workers visibly route the new piping and gearwork around it, leaving it deliberately undisturbed.
**First-second read:** Everything in the background is being changed except one small object that is being carefully protected. The viewer reads "that one thing was kept safe on purpose" before parsing anything else.
**Why it works:** It makes the post's core claim, that the job is safe even though the process isn't, physically visible as a choice being made in real time, rather than a statement someone has to take on faith.
