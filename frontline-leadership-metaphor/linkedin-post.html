Being a front line manager is like trying to get everyone on a bumper car ride to go the same direction and not run into each other. Except the ride is bolted to the back of a truck driving around the city, and you don't know where the next turn is.

Most advice tells frontline managers to communicate better or influence up. That treats it like a skills problem. It is not.

You have real authority over your team. You have none over the truck that team is bolted to, and no amount of skill changes that.

The leaders who burn out are the ones spending their energy trying to steer the truck. The ones who last spend it being honest with their team about which ride they are actually on and why they support it.

What is something your team expects you to control that you actually do not?
