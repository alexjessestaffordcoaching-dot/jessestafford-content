## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A frontline manager is held accountable for outcomes shaped by a decision made three levels above him, and has to face his team with the truth that he never had the authority to change it.

**Context box text:** YOU CONTROL THE TEAM. NOT THE TRUCK.

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is systems-and-accountability content, not a connection or culture piece, so Jesse's expression should read as clear-eyed and grounded rather than warm. He is not defeated. He is a person who has made peace with a hard structural truth and is naming it plainly.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** The bumper car ride bolted to the back of a moving truck, taken directly from the post's central metaphor. The ride is the sphere of control. The truck cab and wheel are the sphere of influence.

**First-second read:** A large flatbed truck fills most of the frame, with a small enclosed bumper-car ring mounted on its bed. A lone figure stands inside that ring, at its edge, looking forward toward the cab far ahead of him. The viewer reads it instantly: he's on board, he runs the ring, but he is nowhere near the wheel.

**Compositional approach:** Truck fills the left two-thirds of the frame, driving through an abstract cityscape rendered in loose charcoal linework. Jesse stands small, inside the bumper-car ring on the truck bed, positioned at the front rail of the ring closest to the cab, one hand resting on the rail, looking toward the driver's cab in the distance. The cab and steering wheel are visible far ahead, small and out of reach, with no driver shown. Negative space (open sky) fills the upper right third of the frame.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide horizontal shot. A large flatbed truck occupies the left two-thirds of the frame, moving through an abstract city rendered in thin charcoal linework in the background. Mounted on the truck's flatbed is a small circular bumper-car ring, scaled clearly smaller than the truck itself. The primary character stands inside that ring, near its front rail, positioned small relative to the truck as a whole — maybe one-eighth the length of the truck bed. He is looking forward and slightly up, toward the empty driver's cab visible far ahead at the front of the truck, which sits outside the ring entirely and is rendered smaller with distance. No driver is visible in the cab. The steering wheel inside it is drawn but unreachable, separated from the ring by the full length of the truck bed. The upper right third of the frame is open sky, left as clean negative space.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. Use a muted warm gray or ochre wash for the truck body so the blue of the shirt stays the clear focal color against it.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed. Standing at the front rail of the bumper-car ring, one hand resting on the rail, weight settled on both feet, looking forward and slightly upward toward the distant cab. Expression: direct and focused, jaw set, calm — a person clearly aware of exactly where the edge of his control is.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The bumper-car ring is rendered with visible schematic detail — gears and rails along its base, exposed mechanics in charcoal linework, consistent with the steampunk background style. Inside the ring, two or three empty bumper cars sit parked, indicating this is a live, functioning system he actually runs. The truck itself is a larger, separate mechanical structure beneath and around the ring — its wheels, chassis, and drive mechanism rendered in more linework, extending well past the ring in both directions. The cab at the far front of the truck bed is small in the frame, its steering wheel drawn but with no hand near it. The background city is abstract and minimal, a few schematic building outlines suggesting motion and scale, kept light so it does not compete with the truck.

CONTEXT BOX: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the bottom of the panel. Text: YOU CONTROL THE TEAM. NOT THE TRUCK.

TONE GUARDRAILS: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene — it does not compete with it.

## Concept 2 — The Room

**Scene dynamic:** Jesse and the team member from the post's opening story, in the moment right after the call — he has to tell her the decision did not change. No equipment, no mechanism. The scene is entirely about the exchange between two people.

**First-second read:** Two people standing in an office doorway, facing each other at equal scale. One has just delivered news the other did not want. The viewer reads it as a real, unresolved conversation — not a confrontation, not a triumph, just an honest moment.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse and the team member positioned at roughly equal scale, facing each other across a few feet of space near a doorway or open office threshold. Jesse's posture is open and steady, not defensive. Her posture shows the moment of the news landing — arms loosely crossed, weight shifted back slightly. Neither is above or below the other in the frame.

The primary character stands on one side of the threshold, facing the camera at a slight angle. A second character stands a few feet away, facing him, at the same visual scale — neither is closer to the camera nor rendered larger. Both are shown roughly waist-up, positioned left-of-center and right-of-center respectively, with a small gap of negative space between them where the conversation is happening. The background room recedes softly behind them with minimal detail, keeping full focus on the two figures and the space between them.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** The absent element. A small toy steering wheel is bolted decoratively to the front of a bumper car — the kind every bumper car has — except this one visibly connects to nothing. The linework shows its cable or shaft trailing off and simply ending, disconnected from any real axle or drive mechanism.

**First-second read:** A close, quiet shot of a hand resting near a toy steering wheel on a bumper car. A beat later the viewer notices the wheel's mechanism is drawn trailing into nothing — it never connected to anything at all. That's the joke and the point at once: the wheel was always decorative.

**Why it works:** It takes the truck metaphor from Concepts 1 and 2 and shrinks it down to the one object everyone has actually touched — a bumper car's toy wheel — making the illusion of control physically visible and slightly funny rather than abstract.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Close, intimate shot, tightly framed on a single bumper car. The primary character is seated in it, visible from the chest up, positioned slightly right of center. His hand rests loosely on a small decorative steering wheel mounted on the dash in front of him. The frame follows the wheel's mechanism downward and to the left with visible charcoal linework — a shaft or cable that runs a short distance and then simply stops in open air, not connected to the car's actual undercarriage or wheels, which are shown faintly at the bottom edge of the frame moving on their own fixed track. Generous negative space surrounds the car, keeping the disconnected mechanism clearly legible.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements rendered faintly in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue) on the primary character's shirt and, more faintly, as an accent wash on the toy wheel itself. Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. A muted warm tone on the bumper car body keeps it visually distinct from the blue shirt and blue-accented wheel.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed, seated in the bumper car with one hand resting lightly on the small wheel, not gripping it. Expression: direct and focused, with a faint, knowing set to the mouth — the look of someone who has just noticed the wheel never did anything, and finds that more clarifying than upsetting.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The bumper car itself is simple and toy-like, rendered with the same patent-drawing precision as the rest of the style. The steering wheel is small, ornamental, clearly a child's-ride detail. Charcoal linework traces its shaft downward, past the dash, and lets it end mid-air with a small gap before the car's real drive mechanism, which is shown along the floor track, unconnected to the wheel entirely. No other characters. No dialogue. Background is kept almost empty — a hint of the ring's rail at the very edge of frame, nothing more.

CONTEXT BOX: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the top of the panel. Text: YOU CONTROL THE TEAM. NOT THE TRUCK.

Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene — it does not compete with it.
