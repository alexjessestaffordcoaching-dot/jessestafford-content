## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** Jesse spent weeks noticing that the automatic words he reached for — "I'm going to argue with the helpdesk," "deal with," "try to" — set the emotional temperature of a room before anyone spoke, and that swapping one phrase at a time ("I'm going to have a conversation with the helpdesk") changed both how he showed up and how people responded.

**Context box text:** YOUR WORDS SET THE ROOM BEFORE YOU SPEAK.

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. This is deliberate, precise communication work, not emotional processing. Jesse's expression is calm, exact, and unhurried — a man making a choice on purpose, with a trace of warmth where the choice pays off.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A worn pocket notebook holding Jesse's running observation log — the negative-connotation phrases he caught himself and others using, some struck through and rewritten. It fits because the post is built on an actual weeks-long practice of writing the phrases down: "Write them down."
**Setting:** A café / break-area table, just after a meeting, an espresso machine on the counter behind.
**First-second read:** A man at a café table crossing a word out of a running list and writing another one next to it.
**Compositional approach:** The open notebook sits on the table at human scale, close to the viewer; Jesse is close and deliberate, pen mid-strike, environment organized around his seat.

**Setting:** A small office meeting room, thermostat on the wall beside the door.
**First-second read:** A man turning a room thermostat from one word toward another, and the people behind him relaxing as he does.
**Why it works:** It takes the article's central metaphor and makes it a control you can watch someone turn — the abstract "temperature of the room" becomes a dial with a needle and a consequence.
