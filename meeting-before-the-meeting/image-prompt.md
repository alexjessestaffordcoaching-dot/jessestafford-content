## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader walks into a meeting believing persuasion still happens in the room, while the actual decision was already made in a private conversation days before.

**Context box text:** THE DECISION IS MADE BEFORE THE MEETING

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused. Jesse's expression is calm, clear-eyed, and deliberate — someone who has worked out how the system actually operates, not someone reacting to it.

---

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A legal pad on Jesse's desk, a short column of names down the left margin, each one checked off or annotated in his own handwriting, next to a desk phone. This is the physical trace of the pre-meeting work — the calls he made to find out who would push back, and what it would take for them to say yes.

**Setting:** Jesse's own office, early morning, before he leaves for the meeting down the hall.

**First-second read:** A worked pad of names and checkmarks next to a phone that's just been set down. Someone has already made every call they needed to make.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse close and deliberate, seated at the desk, the pad and phone positioned in the foreground at human scale — environment organized around his position, the posture of someone who has already done the work and is about to walk into a room that will only confirm it.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important:**
Medium-close shot, 16:9, at desk height. A wooden desk runs across the lower two-thirds of the frame. On it: a yellow legal pad, angled toward the viewer, with four names written down the left margin, each followed by a short checkmark or a scribbled word or two — the visible record of finished calls. Beside the pad, a corded desk phone sits with the handset back in its cradle. Jesse is seated behind the desk, right of center, leaning forward with one forearm resting near the pad, looking down at it — not writing anymore, just confirming what's there. His scale fills roughly half the frame height, seated. Behind him, a window with closed blinds occupies the upper-left, admitting flat early light. Negative space sits in the upper-right corner.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important:**
The desk phone shows its coiled cord and a visible internal spring mechanism at the handset cradle where it clicks down. The legal pad's spiral binding is visible along its top edge. In the negative space of the upper-right corner, faint charcoal schematic linework traces a simple branching conduit — one line splitting into four, echoing the four names on the pad, running up and off the edge of the frame like wiring behind a wall. The S mark is worked into the schematic at the point where the four branch lines converge, rendered as a small cast emblem on the phone's base plate, in charcoal linework.

**Art style spec:**
Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. The desk wood carries a warm amber wash. The legal pad carries a pale yellow wash. Supporting colors stay warm and quiet, subordinate to the blue/white/charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Seated at the desk, leaning forward on one forearm, head tilted down toward the pad. Expression: calm, focused, quietly satisfied — the look of someone confirming work that's already done, not starting it.

**Scene description:** Jesse's own office, not a conference room. Desk, phone, legal pad, closed blinds behind him. The checkmarked names on the pad are the focal detail — legible as marks and short annotations, not as readable words, so no garbled text risk. The phone's handset is seated, cord looping to the base. No other surface in the frame carries lettering; the window blinds, the desk edge, and the wall behind are all blank.

**Context box:** Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: THE DECISION IS MADE BEFORE THE MEETING

**Tone guardrails:** This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The setting is a real place. The charcoal schematic supports the scene from the negative space — it does not float across the middle of the frame and it does not compete with the scene.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Environmental inversion. Jesse and a colleague sit across from each other at a small table in a coffee shop, mid-conversation, coffee cups between them. Through the window behind them, small and distant across the street, a glass office tower rises with one floor lit — the formal boardroom where the "real" meeting will happen. The decision is actually getting made here, at the coffee shop table, not there.

**Setting:** A coffee shop table by the window, across the street from the office tower.

**First-second read:** Two people talking over coffee, with a distant, small office tower visible through the window behind them. The important conversation is the one at the table, not the one waiting across the street.

**Why it works:** The formal boardroom — the place the reader assumes decisions happen — is rendered small and distant through a window, while the real, unremarkable setting of the actual decision fills the frame at full human scale. The reader's assumption about where power lives gets reversed in one image.
