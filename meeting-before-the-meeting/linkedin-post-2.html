I have heard the same concern come out as a question on a phone call and as a position in a conference room.

Say you are in the room when someone raises an objection. Everyone is watching, and backing down now would feel like losing, so they hold the line. The meeting ends with "let's take more time to discuss this."

The same concern, said on a call before the meeting, sounds different. The person says it slowly, trails off, and adds "I am not sure that is even the real issue." It is easier to say out loud when nobody is watching.

A private no is a conversation, and a public no is a vote. So I started calling the person most likely to push back, someone I already talk with regularly, and asking what they are worried about and what it would take to say yes.

It costs time, and it does not always end in a yes. Sometimes the concern is something I can address, and sometimes it was never really about the idea. I would rather find that out on a call.
