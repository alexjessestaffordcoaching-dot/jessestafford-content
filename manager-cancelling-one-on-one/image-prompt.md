**The artifact:** A weekly planner board where the 1-on-1 cards are missing. The trace a cancelled meeting leaves is an empty slot you can see from across the room.
**Setting:** An open-plan office corridor outside the team's glass meeting room.
**First-second read:** Some slots on a schedule are empty and someone is looking at them.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse small and to the side, at a distance, looking toward the gaps instead of touching them.

**Shared mechanism family:** Brass pivots, hinges, spring clips, ratchets and escapements (card rails, door latches, sand-timer gear train).

**Deck read-through:**

1. The invite shows up declined again. | You are already writing the story about what it means, and it is usually the harshest one.
2. One cancellation is noise. | A pattern is information, and it is mostly about your manager's state, not your work.
3. I had a manager with no 1-on-1s. | They never scheduled unless they needed something. So I emailed my priorities and what I would do, and once gave them one hour to stop me.
4. No news is not good news. | A manager fine with no 1-on-1s is either aligned with you or actively disengaged. From outside, it looks the same.
5. 1. Name the pattern without accusing. | Skip "you keep cancelling." Ask if a fixed biweekly slot works. A slot that always happens beats a weekly one that happens half the time.
6. 2. Make the note your 1-on-1. | Send three lines: what I am working on, what I plan to do next, what I need from you.
7. No meeting? Send the note. | What did you assume the last time a 1-on-1 got cancelled, and what would change if you assumed the clearer reading?
