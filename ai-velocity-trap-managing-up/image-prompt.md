## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader is asked whether the AI spend is paying off, answers that the team is not saving time but making far more options than before, and realizes nobody agreed what the extra time was supposed to buy.
**Context box text:** THE TIME GOT FILLED. AGREE WHAT IT SHOULD BUY.
**Emotional tone:** direct-and-focused. The man's expression is level and attentive, a person looking at the evidence and working out what to say, not anxious and not smiling.

---

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** a wall pinned edge to edge with sketch sheets, one for every option the team now makes. It is what "doing more" leaves behind once there is time to fill.
**Setting:** a team design-review room.
**First-second read:** a wall packed with paper and a small man at its foot. Too many options to choose from.
**Compositional approach:** the man is small and low in the frame; the wall fills two-thirds of it, so the viewer feels the volume before reading the box.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important**
Wide shot of a team design-review room, eye-level camera. A cork-covered wall fills the right two-thirds of the frame from floor to ceiling, pinned edge to edge with rows of sketch sheets that overlap at their corners, so densely that almost no cork shows. The man stands in the lower-left third of the frame, small against the wall, one hand in his pocket, head tipped back, looking up at it. A wheeled library ladder on a ceiling rail stands at the left edge of the wall. The upper-left third of the frame stays open wall plane for the schematic. The wall dominates the frame; he reads as outnumbered by options.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important**
Objects and their mechanisms: (1) a rolling library ladder on a ceiling-mounted rail with its wheel carriage, axle and bracket fully visible; (2) a wall clock above the door with its face open to show a visible gear train and escapement; (3) a paper trimmer on a side table with its lever arm, spring and cutting guide visible. The charcoal schematic sits in the upper-left wall plane: loose blueprint linework of a rail, pulley and counterweight system, unpainted, drifting off the edge of the frame. The S mark is cast into the ladder's rail bracket.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Standing in the lower-left third, weight on one leg, head tipped back, expression level and attentive, one hand in his pocket and the other hanging loose.

**Scene description:** A real design-review room: grey carpet, one window with a blind drawn halfway, a plain door, a side table holding the paper trimmer. Each sketch sheet shows simple boxy layouts and product outlines only. Every page, sheet, card, clock face, sign and notebook is blank or unlettered: shapes and tick marks only, no letters or numerals anywhere. Negative space is intentional.

**Context box:** Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme top or bottom of the panel. Text: THE TIME GOT FILLED. AGREE WHAT IT SHOULD BUY.

**Tone guardrails:** This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The setting is a real place. ## Carousel

**Shared setting:** a team design-review studio: one long table, a cork wall, a rail ladder, a window, a plain door.
**Shared mechanism family:** rail and pulley, lever and spring, balance beam: plain working linkage on the ladder, the scale, the card rail and the wall clock.

**Deck read-through:**
1. Leadership asked where the AI savings are. — I told them we are not seeing savings. The tools help, and the team is doing more.
2. The saved time got filled. — Where we used to create 3 concepts to choose from, we now create 15.
3. More options also take more deciding. — Some weeks my team is stuck on what to build, not how. I do not know 15 is worse than 3.
4. More output is not savings. — Leadership expected less time or lower cost. Nothing they watch counts the extra work as value.
5. Agree on outcomes, not output. — Goal progress, outcomes delivered, customer value, savings locked in. One measure everyone agrees on beats four on a slide.
6. Ask what proof would look like. — Ask what they would need to see to say the AI spend paid for itself. Write it down in their words.
7. Agree what the time should buy. — If your leadership asked tomorrow whether AI was paying off, what is the one number you could put in front of them?

**Cold-read check:** (a) Yes: by slide 2 the problem is clear: the team is doing more with the time AI gave back, and leadership sees no savings. (b) Yes: slide 3 gives the turn in plain words (more options take more deciding, and the writer does not know that is worse), and slide 4 states the insight (more output is not savings); neither depends on the post. (c) Yes: by slide 6 the specific Monday action is to ask the director what they would need to see for the AI spend to have paid for itself, write the answer down in their words, and pick one matching measure. (d) Yes: each slide follows from the one before it (question, filled time, stuck deciding, the mismatch, the measures, asking, the number), and no slide needs its picture to be understood.
