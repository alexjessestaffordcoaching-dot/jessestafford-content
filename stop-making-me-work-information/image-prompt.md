## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader opens a presentation of charts from someone who knows the numbers, with no sentence anywhere saying what they mean, and realizes the figuring-out has come back to them.
**Context box text:** LEAD WITH YOUR POINT. PUT THE DETAIL UNDERNEATH.
**Emotional tone:** direct-and-focused. The man's expression is attentive and level, brows slightly drawn, a person reading and working it out rather than annoyed.

---

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** a long table covered in printed chart pages with not one sentence on any of them. It is what the year-end deck leaves on the table once it has to be read.
**Setting:** a plain meeting room with the pages spread across the table.
**First-second read:** a lot of paper, a small person standing at the edge of it. Too much to sort.
**Compositional approach:** the man is small and at the edge of the frame; the pages fill two-thirds of it, so the viewer feels the pile before they read the box.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important**
Wide shot of a plain office meeting room, eye-level camera. A long table fills the right two-thirds of the frame, its whole surface covered with overlapping printed pages, each page a simple chart (bars, lines, pie wedges) with no numerals or lettering. The man stands at the left end of the table, small in the lower-left third of the frame, looking down at the pages with his hands at his sides. The table dominates; he reads as outnumbered by paper. Upper-left and upper-right of the frame stay open wall for the schematic.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important**
Objects and their mechanisms: (1) an articulated desk lamp at the far end of the table showing exposed spring linkage and two pivot joints; (2) a wall clock above the table with its face open to show a visible gear train and escapement; (3) a paper-punch binder at the near corner of the table with its lever arm and cam visible. The charcoal schematic sits in the upper-right wall plane: a loose blueprint-style gear train and linkage drawing, unpainted linework, drifting off the edge of the frame. The S mark is cast into the base of the desk lamp.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Standing at the left end of the table, weight slightly forward, eyes down on the pages, expression attentive and level, hands relaxed at his sides.

**Scene description:** A real meeting room: grey carpet, one window with a blind drawn halfway (blind slats unlettered), a plain wall. The table holds roughly a dozen overlapping chart printouts and one yellow sticky note stuck to the top page that is completely blank. No screens. Every page shows chart shapes only; every page, label and sticky note is unlettered or blank. Negative space is intentional.

**Context box:** Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme top or bottom of the panel. Text: LEAD WITH YOUR POINT. PUT THE DETAIL UNDERNEATH.

**Tone guardrails:** This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The setting is a real place. The charcoal schematic supports the scene from the negative space — it does not float across the middle of the frame and it does not compete with the scene.

Render it in charcoal linework or deep blue (#100299), integrated into the surface it sits on, never as a floating watermark.

**Caption:** Year-end numbers, all charts, and no sentence about what any of them meant. I had to learn the material before I could explain it to my own leadership.

## Carousel

**Shared setting:** one workplace: a private office (desk, two chairs, window with blind, open door) and the meeting room next to it, same palette and furniture throughout.
**Shared mechanism family:** pivots, levers, springs and gear trains on everyday office objects (lamp arm, door closer, wall clock, binder, bulldog clip).

**Deck read-through:**
1. You asked the expert. You got charts. - Now you are the one who has to work out what they mean.
2. The work you delegated came back. - You asked them because they know the numbers. A pile of charts hands the figuring-out back to you.
3. I had to become the expert. - A team member sent year-end charts with no sentences. Before I could pass anything up, I had to learn their work.
4. Order matters more than length. - I will stop reading when I have what I need. Put what I need first.
5. 1. Start with where things stand. - Say the status first: hours per defect moved from 42 to 37 over the last year. (Made-up numbers, for the example.)
6. 2. Then your read, then the ask. - Say what it means to you and what you need. Put the detail underneath for anyone who wants to check it.
7. Would your reader know what you think? - If they only read the subject line and the first three sentences, what would they take away?

**Cold-read check:** (a) Yes. By slide 2 the problem is clear: you asked an expert and the work has come back to you. (b) Yes. Slide 3 gives the story (year-end charts, no sentences, had to learn their work) without needing the post, and slide 4 states the turn, order over length. (c) Yes. By slide 6 the reader has two concrete things: lead with where things stand, then your read and the ask, with detail underneath; slide 7 gives the test to run on the next update. (d) Yes. Each slide follows the one before it; none depends on the picture to make sense.

**Visual beat:** One single sheet in front of him; the stack pushed to the far edge.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important**
Four-by-three frame, eye-level camera, medium shot. The man sits at the desk, one single sheet in front of him, the thick stack pushed to the far edge of the desk and partly out of frame. He reads the sheet with a level, relaxed posture, head up.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important**
A desk lamp with an articulated arm and exposed spring linkage; a wall clock with the face open to a gear train; a lever-and-gear page counter in a paper tray. The charcoal schematic sits in the upper-right wall plane: blueprint linework of a gear train, unpainted. The S mark is cast into the lamp base.

**Art style spec:** Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 4:3

**Color palette:** Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad.

**Primary character:** Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Seated, head up, shoulders relaxed, expression calm and grounded, a person who has what they need.

**Scene description:** The same private office as before: desk, two chairs, wall, window with blind. The single sheet shows a simple chart shape only. All pages and surfaces are blank or unlettered.

**Context box:** None. This panel carries no text of any kind — no caption box, no labels, no lettering. Every letterable surface is blank.

**Tone guardrails:** This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The setting is a real place. The charcoal schematic supports the scene from the negative space — it does not float across the middle of the frame and it does not compete with the scene.
