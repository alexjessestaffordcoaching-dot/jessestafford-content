Why do smart people send charts with no story? Because then someone else has to make the decision.

There are three reasons, and that is only one of them. School taught most of us to build toward a point, the way a story builds to its ending. Almost nobody was taught business writing, where the answer goes first.

Then there is pride. A presentation full of charts shows how much work went in, and it is fair to want that seen.

But committing to a view means someone might disagree. Handing over the detail feels safer, even though it leaves the reader with the hard part.

If you send the detail because making a decision feels risky, that is exactly what I work through with leaders in coaching.
