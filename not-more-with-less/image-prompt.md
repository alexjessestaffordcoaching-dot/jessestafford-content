## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** After a headcount loss, a leader finds a way to say what actually changes — not a general directive, the specific process that ends — and in doing so gives the team something real to hold onto rather than an assumption they had been dreading.

**Context box text:** NOT MORE EFFORT. THE SPECIFIC THING THAT CHANGES.

**Emotional tone:** Quiet and grounded. This is not a dramatic confrontation — it is a calm, deliberate act of naming something out loud that nobody else has named. Jesse's expression should read as settled, composed, unrushed. The relief comes from the specificity, not the performance.

## Concept 1 — The Lever

**Visual metaphor:** A control panel/switchboard built for five operator stations, now running on two. Jesse is mid-action, pulling one specific lever outward — deliberately disengaging it rather than pushing the remaining levers harder. The point is not that the machine is in crisis. The point is that the person at it knows exactly which input to stop.

**First-second read:** A system built for five hands, staffed at two, with the same volume still moving through it — and the one person there is removing a step, not straining to run all of them.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse close, deliberate, positioned at the panel with the environment organized around him. The panel fills the right two-thirds of the frame. Jesse stands at its edge in the lower-left, one hand on a single outward-pulled lever, three unlit unmanned stations visible behind him.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide shot, single panel. A five-station control panel/switchboard fills the right two-thirds of the frame, mounted on an exposed mechanical wall structure. Only two of the five stations show active lit gauges; the other three sit dark and unmanned. All five gauges/dials still read full volume, needles pinned toward the high end, pipes or belts feeding into the panel from off-frame at consistent, undiminished volume. Jesse stands in the lower-left third of the frame, at moderate scale, close to the panel and facing it at a three-quarter angle. His hand is on a single lever that is visibly pulled outward, disengaged, distinct from the others — not pushing on the panel, but removing one input from it. Negative space fills the upper-left corner. Nothing else occupies the frame.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. Supporting colors are allowed for depth and warmth but must not compete with the blue/white/charcoal triad. The two active/manned station gauges can carry a warm amber or brass accent to read as "still under load"; the three dark stations stay charcoal and white only.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Standing close to the panel, weight forward and engaged, one hand gripping a disengaged lever mid-pull. Expression calm and grounded — steady, direct focus on the panel, composed, no strain in the face or shoulders.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The five-station panel is the clear focal element, patent-drawing precision in its linework — visible gears, pipes, and exposed brass fittings behind the gauges. Two stations glow faintly active; three sit dark, their levers idle. All gauge needles read the same high volume regardless of how many stations are staffed, making clear the workload has not shrunk. Jesse's lever is caught mid-motion, pulled distinctly out of alignment with the others — the single deliberate change against an otherwise unchanged system. Background steampunk piping threads along the upper edges of the frame. Ample white negative space in the upper-left.

CONTEXT BOX: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: NOT MORE EFFORT. THE SPECIFIC THING THAT CHANGES.

TONE GUARDRAILS: Grounded, human, specific. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate. Not inspirational abstraction. The lever being removed is not a dramatic act — it is a deliberate and quiet one.

## Concept 2 — The Room

**First-second read:** A team space where the gaps from the departures are visible — chairs tucked in, monitors still there, no one sitting — and Jesse is not standing apart from all of it but in the middle of the same space, saying the specific thing.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse and two remaining teammates at equal scale, eye level. Two or three empty desks visible in the same row, unoccupied but not staged as a memorial. Jesse positioned left-of-center, facing the two teammates on the right, one hand open mid-gesture.

Jesse is positioned left-of-center, angled toward the two teammates on the right, one hand open at chest height mid-gesture as if explaining something specific — not delivering a speech. The two teammates are seated or half-standing, attentive, at equal eye level to Jesse.

Jesse stands in the open floor space in front of the row, at the same eye level and scale as his teammates. Background steampunk piping and exposed structural beams run along the upper frame. ## Concept 3 — The Machine and the Gear

**Unexpected angle:** An elaborate, dormant AI-tool contraption sits center-frame — silent, nothing moving. Off to the side, small and unglamorous, Jesse is mid-swap on a plain gear, and that small mechanism is the only thing generating motion in the frame. The impressive machine is decoration. The specific process change is what is actually running the system.

**First-second read:** The eye goes first to the large, impressive-looking machine — then registers it is completely still, while the real motion comes from the small, unremarkable gear swap in the corner.

**Why it works:** The post's argument in one image: the AI tool alone does not close a headcount gap. The plain, deliberate process change is what actually moves things forward.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide shot, single panel. An elaborate, ornate AI-tool contraption — dense with dials, indicator lights rendered as small painted circles, polished brass fittings — dominates the right two-thirds of the frame, elevated slightly on a pedestal. Entirely still: no motion lines, no steam, no engaged gears, lights unlit. In the lower-left corner, small and modest in scale, Jesse crouches at a plain, unglamorous gear mechanism, actively swapping one gear for another. Motion lines and a thin wisp of steam rise only from this small mechanism — the only active movement in the frame. The scale contrast is deliberate: the impressive object is large and idle; the plain object is small and alive.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color bleeds slightly outside ink lines. Wet-edge textures required. No digital gradients. No glow effects. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements in charcoal linework. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. The large idle AI contraption can carry a cool, muted brass/grey wash to read as inert and decorative; the small active gear mechanism and its steam wisp can carry a warmer amber accent to read as the one thing actually alive in the frame.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed. Crouched at the small mechanism, sleeves visibly pushed up, one hand mid-motion fitting a gear into place. Expression calm and grounded — focused and unhurried, entirely unbothered by the large idle machine beside him.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The large AI contraption sits inert on its pedestal, gleaming and ornate but doing nothing. Jesse's small mechanism, by contrast, is plain, worn, and clearly in motion: a single gear being swapped, a thin curl of steam, a faint hum implied through short motion lines. The size disparity is deliberate and slightly exaggerated. Background steampunk piping ties the two mechanisms together as part of the same larger system, without suggesting the AI machine is connected to anything productive.
