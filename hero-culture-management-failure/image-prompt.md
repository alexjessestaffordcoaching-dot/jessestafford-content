## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A manager watches every hard question route back to one engineer, recognizes the risk, and still has a good reason every week not to fix it — until the engineer leaves and takes the knowledge with them.

**Context box text:** THE KNOWLEDGE LIVES IN ONE HEAD

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused — this is a systems and accountability topic. Jesse's expression across the hero concepts is steady and candid: the look of someone who has watched this play out more than once and knows exactly what it costs. Concept 2, the career conversation, shifts to calm and grounded — that moment demands composure, not just clarity.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A task board wall covered in sticky notes, nearly all of them in the "In Progress" column marked with the same handwritten initials. The board is the routing system. Its density tells the story without a caption.

**Setting:** A team standup area in an open engineering office — the wall with the board, a row of monitors and standing desks visible in the background, one or two colleagues visible at their workstations with their backs to the camera.

**First-second read:** A wall of work, almost all flowing to one name. Before the context box, the viewer reads: everything points to one person.

**Compositional approach:** Jesse stands left-of-center at medium scale, looking toward the board. The board occupies the right two-thirds of the frame. The concentration of sticky notes on one column is the focal element.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important:**
Wide shot. A large task board fills the right two-thirds of the frame — sticky notes arranged in columns (blank headers), the middle column dense with notes, almost all marked with the same two-letter handwritten initials, the other columns sparse. Jesse stands to the left at medium-small scale, slightly turned toward the board, looking at it. His posture is steady — not alarmed, not performing concern: the look of someone seeing clearly. Two colleagues are visible further back in the frame at standing desks, shoulders turned away. The left margin holds negative space — the schematic layer lives there.

**MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important:**
The sticky notes are the routing mechanism — small arrows drawn between some of them, converging toward the dense column. The board frame has basic mechanical joinery (exposed pin hinges, corner brackets) visible in charcoal linework. In the upper-left negative space above the board and Jesse, a charcoal schematic network routing diagram: nodes connected by directed arrows, all converging to one central node. The S mark is worked into the central convergence node of that schematic, in charcoal linework.

**Art style spec:**
Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

**Color palette:**
Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash — never solid black fill. The charcoal schematic layer stays unpainted linework. The sticky notes carry warm amber and pale yellow watercolor washes to contrast the blue/white/charcoal triad without competing with it.

**Primary character:**
Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed. Standing left-of-center, body angled slightly toward the board, expression steady and candid — looking, not performing.

A team standup area. The task board wall dominates the right side of the frame — columns of sticky notes, the center column far denser than the others. The sticky notes are yellow and amber, hand-drawn marks on each; the dense column's notes all share the same two-letter initial mark. The board frame is charcoal-colored with exposed corner hardware.
