The most comfortable sentence on a team is "just ask <name>." It is also the one that tells you where your risk is.

On one of my teams, a single engineer was the only person who understood a piece of shared code that every product for one market depended on. Every request for it waited in one line.

I saw it early. The delays were already there, and I could not take that engineer's hours to teach anyone. Nothing changed, and on any given day it felt like having a very good engineer.

That comfort is the part that took me longest to see. It looks good for you and feels safe for them, and the team carries the risk without anyone naming it.

What would stop first on your team if your go-to person was out for two weeks?
