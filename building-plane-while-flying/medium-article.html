How to Lead Your Team When You Are Still Building the Plan

Quick Bridge: Your organization is in the middle of transformation and you do not have a complete answer to give your team yet. The instinct is to wait until you do. This post is about why that instinct costs you the team's confidence, what the difference is between not having the details and not having the direction, and what it looks like to lead forward when the plan is still being built.

---

I took my current role knowing I was not ready for it.

The scope was significantly bigger than anything I had managed before. No title change. No additional pay. I was getting complacent in my previous position, and the challenge seemed like the right kind of discomfort.

Three years in, it is one of the most significant growth periods of my career. I am leading a larger team, still learning, and building skills that matter for what comes next.

None of that happened because I waited until I had a clear picture of how to do it. I moved before I had that picture.

---

That is how most meaningful moves work.

I was never fully ready for any of my big career growth positions. I thought I was ready for my first manager role. I was not. I did not think I was ready to become a Director. I figured it out when I got there.

The lesson I have carried forward is this: you will not be ready for the role until you are in it. That is not a failure of preparation. It is how leadership growth works.

That same truth applies to what you are in the middle of right now.

---

Your organization is changing. The plan is not finished. Some of the decisions above you have not been made yet. Your team is asking what this means for them, and you do not have a complete answer.

The instinct is to wait until you do.

That instinct is going to cost you.

---

What Your Team Is Watching For

When a leader goes quiet during transformation, the team fills the silence. They do not fill it with patience. They fill it with the worst version of the story they can imagine.

What your team is watching for is not the complete plan. They are watching to see if you know where you are going.

Those are different things.

A leader who does not have every detail but moves with conviction communicates something specific: the direction is set, and we are working through the execution together. A leader who waits for perfect information before saying anything communicates the opposite. The team reads that as uncertainty about the direction itself.

Your team can work with incomplete details. They have a harder time following someone who seems to be waiting for permission to lead.

---

The Distinction That Matters

There is a difference between being uncertain about the details and being uncertain about the direction. Leaders who confuse these two end up in one of two places. They perform confidence they do not have, or they go quiet while they wait for more information. Neither works.

Separate them.

You know where you are going. You know the reason behind it. You know what the team is working toward and what is not changing. What you do not have yet is the exact timeline, some downstream decisions, and a few process details still being resolved.

Lead with the first. Acknowledge the second. That sequence matters.

Direction comes first. Uncertainty is a detail, not the headline.

"We are moving toward X. Here is why I am confident that is the right direction. I do not have every step figured out yet. Here is what we are doing next." That is a complete communication. It does not require having all the answers.

---

Make Decisions. Own Them.

The other half of this is harder.

Waiting for certainty is how leaders end up not deciding. And not deciding is a decision. It just looks like absence.

The team watches what you do, not just what you say. If you are communicating a direction but hedging on every call that requires judgment, the communication does not hold. Your actions tell the team whether you actually believe what you are saying.

This is difficult. Making decisions without complete information is uncomfortable. The natural pull is to delay until things get clearer.

The team needs to see you move. Not recklessly. Not without listening. They need to see that you can function under uncertainty. That you are not paralyzed by it.

Make the call. Adjust when you get new information. Say out loud that you adjusted, and why.

Visible adjustment is not weakness. It is what a leader who is paying attention looks like.

---

Repeat the Direction Far More Than Feels Necessary

There is an old saying that people need to hear something seven times before they understand it. That saying forgets to add: it is seven times per person. With a team of ten, you are looking at roughly seventy repetitions of the same message before it lands consistently.

What feels like overcommunicating is usually the first time some people are actually hearing it.

Vary how you say it. Use your team meeting, your one-on-ones, the hallway. Same direction, different words.

This is where leaders underestimate the gap. You have been living with this transformation for weeks. You have processed it, asked questions about it, thought about it at 6am. Your team is starting that process each time you speak about it. The fortieth time feels redundant to you. It is still new for half your team.

---

The Alternative Is Not Waiting It Out

The alternative to building the plane while flying it is not landing safely and rebuilding from the ground. Landing is not an option. Landing means stopping the business.

You are already airborne because the business took off before this transformation started. You would not be going through a change this significant if you were not already moving. The plane is in the air because the company has to keep making money. That is why you are building while you fly.

What your team needs is someone who can function at that altitude. Not someone performing certainty they do not have. Not someone waiting for the ground to stop moving before they take a step.

Acknowledge what you do not know. Be clear about where you are going. Make the next call.

You do not need all the answers to lead through this. You need the conviction to move with the ones you have.

---

Monday Action

Before your next team meeting, write down two things: the direction you are confident about, and one or two things you do not have resolved yet.

Start the meeting by stating the direction clearly. Mention the gaps briefly. Just what they are and what you are doing about them. Then keep moving.

Not a speech. Not a strategic overview. Just the direction, the honest gaps, and the next step.
