## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader keeps his team moving through an organizational change whose plan is still half-built — leading with the direction he is sure of and naming the gaps out loud, instead of going quiet until every answer arrives.

**Context box text:** LEAD WITH THE DIRECTION. NAME THE GAPS.

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused, with grounded steadiness. Jesse is not alarmed by the incompleteness around him — he is in motion, someone who has decided rather than someone hoping. In Concept 3 the steadiness reads a little more as calm-and-grounded: hands settled, unhurried, no strain.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A small aircraft in mid-build inside a maintenance hangar — one engine cowling off, a wing panel removed — being walked toward the open hangar door anyway. A transformation still in progress leaves exactly this: the thing half-apart and already moving. It fits because the post's whole frame is "building the plane while flying it," and the alternative — landing — means stopping the business.
**Setting:** An aircraft maintenance hangar with the main door rolled open to a taxiway in daylight.
**First-second read:** A plane that clearly is not finished, and someone moving it toward the door while the crew keeps working on it.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse at human scale between the plane and the open door, mid-stride toward the daylight, one hand pointing out toward the taxiway, talking back to two people still working on the exposed engine. The unfinished plane fills the left and center; the open door and light are the right.

Jesse is in the captain's seat flying it, hands on the yoke, a team member flying alongside in the right seat, both calm, both looking forward at clear sky.
**Setting:** The cockpit of an airliner, in daylight flight, view forward through the windscreen.
**First-second read:** Two pilots calmly flying a plane whose dashboard is visibly not finished.
**Why it works:** It is the post's exact phrase — building the plane while flying it — made literal, and the incongruity (nobody flies an unfinished plane) is what makes the reader stop. The calm on both faces reframes it from crisis to how the work actually gets done.
