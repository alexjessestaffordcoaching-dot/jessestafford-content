When your team pushes back after a change, they are not challenging your decision. They are telling you something was taken from them.

I changed a QA team's goals from individual metrics to team-level metrics. The complaints started immediately. It is not fair. There is nothing we can do.

We cannot control this. My first instinct was to explain the rationale. I had already explained it. Same complaint the next week.

What I was reading as resistance was grief. Goals are not just targets. They are how people measure whether they are doing their job well. Change the goal and you cut off that signal.

A competent person can no longer tell whether they are competent. That is a real loss, and it produces a response that sounds like stubbornness but is not.

When we keep explaining the rationale, we are solving the wrong problem. What the team needs first is to feel heard. Ask why it feels unfair. Listen. Do not defend the decision in that conversation. Do not change your decision either.

If your team has been pushing back on a change for weeks, what have you tried that actually moved things forward?
