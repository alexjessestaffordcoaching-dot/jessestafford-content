A client rewrote the same check-in four times. Every version ended with "just checking this is on your radar."

Each one asked her director to reassure her, and he had a full week with no room for that. By the fourth version she was deleting it before she finished reading it.

She stopped checking in. She sent a short update that opened with the one decision he needed to make by Thursday, and put everything else after it.

In my experience a stretched manager reads for about half a minute, then passes on whatever they can repeat. If the thing they must act on sits at the bottom, it never gets there. The ask belongs on line one. It will not land with every manager, but it costs less than another check-in.

If your updates bury the ask at the bottom, that is the kind of thing I work through with leaders in coaching.
