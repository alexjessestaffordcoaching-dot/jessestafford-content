LinkedIn Post 2: How to Give Your Team Real Ownership Without Everything Falling Apart

Platform: LinkedIn | Target: 3-5 paragraphs, 30-second read Status: DRAFT. Not approved.

There is an old saying that people need to hear a goal seven times, and it leaves out that the count is per person. With ten people on a team, that is seventy times.

In a 1-on-1 I was about to say, for the fifth time that month, that we wanted fewer surprises from other departments. It felt like talking down to someone who surely had it by now, and I almost skipped it.

I said it differently instead: "If someone from another team asks you for something, I want to hear about it before it gets urgent." Their question back, whether I wanted to hear about the small requests too, stung a little, because it showed they had been holding a narrower version of the goal.

I can't tell you seven is the right number, but I treat ten people as ten separate counts. You get tired of hearing yourself say it long before they do, and I have found that is the point.

If you have told your team the goal and still wonder why decisions drift from it, that is the kind of thing leaders work through with me in coaching.
