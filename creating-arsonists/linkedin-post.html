The people creating emergencies on your team are not doing it because they like chaos. They are doing it because urgency is the only thing that has worked.

I coach technology leaders who are exhausted by this pattern. Everything feels like a fire and the fires never stop.

What they are experiencing is not bad luck. It is a system they built one response at a time.

When early flags disappear into the backlog and crises get the full room, the team learns to wait for the crisis.

When the person who stayed up to save a deployment gets thanked in standup and the person who flagged the risk four weeks earlier gets nothing, the team learns what is worth doing.

Nobody decided to play the system. They just stopped doing the thing that never moved anything.

The last three times someone on your team got recognized, who was it? Was it the rescue or the prevention?
