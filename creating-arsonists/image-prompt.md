## Core Visual Idea

The post is not about bad actors — it is about a recognition gap. The team learns what is worth doing by watching what gets noticed. Every time the rescue gets the spotlight and the prevention gets nothing, you run a training session. The image should evoke: the structural invisibility of doing things right. Not chaos. Not a villain. The absence of recognition for the thing that never had to become a fire.

---

## Concept 1: The Recognition Gap

**Scene:** A standup meeting or brief team gathering. One person stands at the front, receiving attention — gesturing, clearly being thanked, well-lit and central in the frame. Colleagues are facing them. Off to one side, slightly out of focus but visible, another person sits at a laptop — quietly completing something, head down, unacknowledged. Both things are happening at the same time. The viewer sees both.

**Mood:** The recognition moment is warm and collegial — not hostile. That is what makes the contrast effective. The viewer understands the system without any labels.

**Color palette:** Warm ambient light on the recognition group. The solo figure is in cooler, dimmer light — the same room, different spotlight. No dramatic contrast; just a visible difference in who the light finds.

**Do not include:** Literal fire, flames, anything adversarial, anything that makes the unacknowledged person look sad or resentful. They are focused, not dejected.

**Suggested crop:** Landscape. The recognition group in the left-center of frame; the solo figure visible but secondary in the right background.

---

## Concept 2: The Training Session

**Scene:** A leader at a whiteboard or in a presentation posture — mid-gesture, clearly explaining something. The expression is genuine and well-intentioned. The whiteboard or slide behind them shows a loop: crisis → all-hands response → recognition → (back to) crisis. The leader is not aware they are teaching the loop. One or two team members in the background are taking notes.

**Mood:** Good intent, unintended lesson. No villain. The training session is happening without anyone knowing it is a training session. The viewer sees the cycle the leader cannot.

**Color palette:** Clean office aesthetics, whiteboard lighting, neutral tones. The cycle diagram on the board should be legible as a loop but not labeled — the visual shape communicates it.

**Do not include:** Anything aggressive or accusatory. The leader is well-intentioned. The team is engaged. The problem is structural, not personal.

**Suggested crop:** Landscape or square. Leader fills the left half of frame; whiteboard or slide visible on the right.

---

## Concept 3: The Invisible Fix

**Scene:** A close-up of a desk with two sides. Left side: a clear workspace — laptop closed or task-complete indicator visible, a coffee cup, an orderly stack. No alerts. Someone's hand resting lightly. Right side: scattered papers, a phone mid-call or in hand, a screen showing alert indicators (red or amber lights). The left side is where something was prevented before it became a crisis. No one called. The right side is the fire. The contrast is the entire argument.

**Mood:** Matter-of-fact. Neither side is dramatic. The viewer understands the cost without text. Prevention is invisible; the rescue is the thing in the frame.

**Color palette:** Neutral desk tones. Right side has warm red/amber from alert indicators. Left side is clean and quiet. The contrast is in the state of the surface, not dramatic lighting.

**Do not include:** Faces. Specific identifiable content on screens. Anything that makes the "fire" side look like a failure — the point is that both sides are normal, and one of them gets attention.

**Suggested crop:** Landscape. Clean left-to-right split. The two desk states fill the frame equally.

Concept 2 works best as a social share — it is the most immediately legible metaphor (the leader teaching something they do not realize they are teaching).
