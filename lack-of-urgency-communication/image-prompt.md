## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader hands a team the deadline but not the reason it matters, and reads the team's flat pace as low motivation instead of a missing piece of information. Urgency spreads once the why is actually handed over, not before.
**Context box text:** URGENCY IS SOMETHING YOU HAND SOMEONE, IN FULL
**Emotional tone:** Direct and clear-eyed, not remorseful. This is a leader naming a mechanism he now understands, not confessing a failure. Jesse's expression should read as settled recognition, someone who has made this exact correction before.

## Concept 1 — The System

**Visual metaphor:** A small mechanical gear assembly (the "task") is turning, but slowly and unevenly, its pressure line disconnected from a large pressure tank behind it (the "why," the reason, the fuel). The tank is full and clearly pressurized, but its feed line dangles unconnected a few inches from the gear housing. The fix is obvious and small: connect the line.
**First-second read:** A mechanism straining, half-powered, right next to a full tank it is not hooked up to. The eye reads "the power is right there, it just was not connected" before reading the context box.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse stands in the lower third, at moderate scale, one hand near the disconnected feed line, not yet attaching it, looking toward it with recognition rather than urgency. The straining gear assembly sits in the foreground; the full pressure tank looms slightly larger in the mid-background so the disconnect reads as the obvious problem.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Wide shot, 16:9. In the foreground, lower-center of the frame, a small gear-and-piston assembly turns unevenly, visibly straining, connected to a simple task-wheel that moves in slow, uneven jerks rather than a smooth spin. A few inches behind and above it, a large pressure tank sits fully built and clearly pressurized (gauge needle in the high range, faint steam venting from a safety valve), its main feed line hanging loose and disconnected a short distance from the gear housing's intake valve. Jesse stands beside the gear assembly, lower-left third, at moderate scale, one hand resting near the disconnected feed line without yet attaching it, his gaze directed at the gap between the line and the intake valve. Negative space above the tank is intentional — do not fill the upper third of the frame.

ART STYLE: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Background: clean white paper texture (#ffffff) with steampunk mechanical elements — objects have exposed mechanics — rendered in charcoal linework as abstract environmental texture. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

COLOR PALETTE: Primary watercolor wash: #100299 (deep blue). Canvas and negative space: #ffffff. Linework and shadows: #333333. Shadows are a semi-transparent charcoal watercolor wash, never solid black fill. The pressure tank carries a warm amber wash to read as full and active. The straining gear assembly stays mostly charcoal-linework with only faint desaturated blue, to read as under-powered until it is connected.

PRIMARY CHARACTER: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Relaxed, unposed, never stiff or formal. Standing beside the gear assembly, weight settled, one hand held near the disconnected feed line, expression direct and clear-eyed, the look of someone who has just spotted the obvious missing connection rather than someone panicking about it.

SCENE DESCRIPTION: The uneven, straining gear assembly is the first focal point. The full pressure tank and its dangling, unconnected feed line is the second, and the gap between the two objects is the visual argument of the whole image. Background mechanical texture (pipes, small secondary gauges) stays light in the corners and does not compete with the two focal objects.

CONTEXT BOX: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the extreme bottom of the panel. Text: URGENCY IS SOMETHING YOU HAND SOMEONE, IN FULL

TONE GUARDRAILS: This image should feel like two people talking through a real problem in plain language. Not a motivational poster. Not corporate (no handshakes, whiteboards, or team huddles). Not inspirational abstraction. Grounded, human, and specific. The mechanical background supports the scene, it does not compete with it.

A second, larger rolled document, clearly labeled with dense handwriting (the reasoning behind the date), still sits untouched on the table between them. The team member holds the folded paper but is looking at the table, not at Jesse, posture neutral rather than urgent.
**First-second read:** One person just received something small, but their attention is on a second, larger item still sitting on the table that nobody has picked up yet. The viewer reads "something is still on the table" before reading the context box.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse and the team member sit across a simple worktable from each other, roughly equal scale, peer framing rather than hierarchy. The folded date-paper is mid-handoff between them. The larger rolled document sits closer to Jesse's side of the table, still untouched, slightly more in focus than the background so the eye catches it as the missing piece.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** A fully fueled lantern sits unlit on a workbench, clearly capable and ready, while a small lit match sits unused in a matchbox a short distance away, never brought over. The lantern is not broken. It was simply never given the spark.
**First-second read:** A ready lantern, dark, beside a lit match that never made the short trip to it. The viewer gets it instantly: the capability was always there, only the spark was withheld.
**Why it works:** It flips the expected framing from "the team lacks drive" to "the spark was never handed over," which is the exact reversal the post argues for. The lantern (the team's effort and readiness) is not the problem. The missing, close-at-hand match (the leader's withheld reason) is.
