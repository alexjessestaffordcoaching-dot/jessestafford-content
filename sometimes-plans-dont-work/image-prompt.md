## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A technical leader finishes real work, assumes it will speak for itself, and only realizes later that it never reached the people deciding his career — because he never showed it to them.

**Context box text:** GOOD WORK NOBODY SEES DOESN'T MOVE CAREERS

**Emotional tone:** Direct and focused — this is strategy and decision-making content. Jesse's expression is clear-eyed and deliberate, not stressed or anxious. He has figured something out and is acting on it.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** A neat stack of finished project write-ups sitting in a wire mesh out-tray on his desk — work that is completely done and never left the room. The tray is the physical proof that "done" and "seen" are two different things.
**Setting:** His home office at night — desk against the wall, lamp on, everyone else in the house asleep.
**First-second read:** A man at a desk late at night, looking at a stack of finished papers sitting in an out-tray that has clearly not moved in days.
**Compositional approach:** Jesse close and deliberate, seated directly at the desk, the tray positioned centrally in front of him at close range — this is a clarity/decision moment, not a distant one.

### Full Expanded Prompt

COMPOSITION — most important: Medium-wide shot of a home office at night. The desk sits slightly right of center, running parallel to the back wall. Jesse is seated at the desk in the lower-right two-thirds of the frame, filling roughly 40% of the frame height, shown from the side at a three-quarter angle. He is leaned forward slightly, forearms on the desk, looking down at a two-tier wire mesh out-tray positioned directly in front of him, close range, human scale. A laptop sits open to his left, screen angled toward him. Negative space occupies the upper-left third of the frame and the ceiling plane above the desk — this is where the schematic layer sits. The window behind the desk is dark, curtain half-drawn.

MECHANICAL ENVIRONMENT — second most important: The wire mesh out-tray sits on two angled metal legs with a visible pivot hinge where the tray tilts forward — the working mechanism of a tray literally built to let things slide out, currently doing nothing. Beside it, an articulated desk lamp with two visible elbow joints and a counterweight spring at the base casts the only light in the room. The laptop screen shows a blank, unlettered draft-message window — a plain rectangle with a cursor, no legible text. A small brass desk stapler sits at the corner of the desk, its spring hinge visible at the back. The charcoal schematic linework occupies the upper-left wall and ceiling plane, rendered as a thin blueprint-style flow path arcing from the desk toward a door frame implied at the far left edge of the composition — unpainted, blueprint annotation only. The S mark is cast into the round base plate of the articulated desk lamp, rendered in fine charcoal linework.

Art style spec: Crisp, uniform charcoal ink outlines (#333333), medium line weight throughout. Comic book precision — not sterile flat-art, not heavy superhero cross-hatching. Lines should suggest a technical patent drawing or a well-crafted indie comic panel. Loose, organic watercolor fills — visibly hand-painted. Color intentionally bleeds slightly outside the ink lines or leaves small white gaps at boundaries. Wet-edge textures and transparency variations are required — these are features, not errors. No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Base canvas: clean white paper texture (#ffffff). Single panel.

Relaxed, unposed — never stiff or formal. Seated, leaning forward with forearms resting on the desk, looking down at the out-tray with a focused, clear-eyed expression — the look of someone who has just noticed something obvious in hindsight.

**Why it works:** It turns "the environment is telling you something, most people just aren't gathering it" from a line in the post into a literal, legible action — tuning past noise for a signal that was there the whole time.
