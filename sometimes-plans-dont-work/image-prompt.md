## Concept 1 — The Fork

**Visual metaphor:** Two diverging paths rendered as schematic architectural cross-sections. The left path is wider and marked with conventional upward-progress indicators — a higher level label, a broader opening. The right path is narrower at the entry point but expands as it extends forward, opening into a wider space beyond. Jesse stands at the fork, body angled toward the narrower right path. He is not looking back at the conventional one. He has already made the assessment.

**First-second read:** A figure at a fork in a schematic system, facing the smaller path that opens wider ahead. Viewer reads: someone who chose correctly, even when the obvious path pointed a different direction.

**Compositional approach:** Wide shot. The fork occupies 70% of the frame. The two paths diverge from Jesse's position in the lower-center of the frame — one going upper-left (conventional path), one going upper-right (chosen path). Jesse is at the base of the fork, slightly right of center, body facing the right-side path. The right path widens approximately two-thirds of the way through the frame, suggesting the opening ahead.

### Full Expanded Prompt

The right path extends upper-right: it is narrower at the entry point but expands noticeably approximately two-thirds of the way through its visible length, opening into a wider rendered space. Jesse stands at the junction point — the base of the fork — positioned slightly right of center. His body is angled toward the right path. He is not looking back at the left. His posture communicates a decision already made: calm, forward-oriented, ready to move.

No digital gradients. No glow effects. No perfectly aligned fills. Mechanical background elements suggest systems and structure without being literal or dominant. 16:9 aspect ratio, single panel.

The conventional left-side path uses warm amber tones — not wrong, just a different choice. Jesse's blue shirt is the only primary-blue human element in the frame.

Primary character: Man in his early 40s. Short buzz-cut hair. Neatly trimmed short beard and mustache. Brown eyes. V-neck long sleeve shirt in deep blue (#100299) watercolor wash. Charcoal pants. Standing at the base of the fork, body angled toward the right path. Slightly forward-leaning. Expression: deliberate and settled — he has assessed both options and made his choice. Not uncertain, not looking back.

Scene description: Schematic cross-section of a forking architectural system — two diverging pathways rendered with structural depth, measurement markers, and directional indicators. The left path is labeled with conventional career-progression notation (schematic lines suggesting "upward," level indicators). The right path opens wider as it extends, with the expanding section rendered in deeper blue watercolor — the widening is the visual payoff. Jesse stands at the base of the fork, small relative to the system around him (approximately 15 percent of frame height), body angled toward the right path. No other figures. Steampunk mechanical details in the background — exposed components, structural notation — reinforce the idea of systems that can be read and navigated.

Context box: Uppercase sans-serif text inside a solid white (#ffffff) rectangle with a sharp charcoal (#333333) border. Placed at the bottom of the panel. Text: CAREERS ARE 25% LUCK. THE REST IS YOURS.

This is the Show Your Work moment: the plans being shared before the work is finished.

**First-second read:** Two figures mid-exchange — one handing a document, one reaching to receive it. Viewer reads: information being shared in the moment it matters, not held until the end.

**Compositional approach:** Medium shot. Jesse occupies the left third of the frame, extending a schematic document toward the center. The receiving figure occupies the center-right, partially turned, hands reaching toward the document. The document is the visual center of the frame — held at mid-height, both figures' attention directed toward it.

## Concept 3 — The Reframe

**Unexpected angle:** Scale inversion + the absent/separated element. A large mechanical gauge is divided into two visible sections: three-quarters accessible and fully rendered, one-quarter behind a transparent glass panel. Jesse stands small at the base of the gauge, facing the accessible three-quarters. His back is to the glass-enclosed quarter. He is not looking at it, not frustrated by it. He has simply turned away from what he cannot reach and is directing his full attention to what he can.

**First-second read:** A small figure standing in front of a large divided gauge, oriented away from the inaccessible portion and toward the larger accessible area. Viewer reads: someone who has made a clear-eyed decision about where to direct their energy.

**Why it works:** The 25/75 split is rendered spatially and literally. The glass panel is the visual shorthand for "you cannot reach this and that is fine." Jesse's turned back to the 25% is the reframe — not defeat, not frustration. A choice.
