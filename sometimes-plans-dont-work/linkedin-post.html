Most technical leaders assume their best work will be noticed. It usually is not.

Not because the work is not good. Because the people making decisions about your career cannot see it.

Showing your work is not self-promotion. It is translation. Your manager speaks business language: risk, delivery, velocity, impact. They do not understand your technical context. They depend on you to close that gap.

The sequence:

1. Share what you are working toward before the project starts 2. Translate updates as the work moves: what shipped, what changed, why it matters 3. Close the loop when done, and name what you are picking up next

Your career is roughly 25% luck. The project that got cancelled. The advocate who moved on. The reorg that buried three years of solid work.

The other 75% is yours. Showing your work is the most important behavior in that 75%.

What would change if the right people actually knew what you have been building?
