## Shared Elements

**Core situation:** A leader's strongest engineer is faster than ever with AI and quietly drained, and asks to go back to doing part of the work by hand, because steering a set of agents all day leaves her nothing she can point to and call her own.
**Context box text:** BUILDING PAYS YOU BACK. STEERING SPENDS YOU DOWN.
**Emotional tone:** Calm and grounded. Jesse's expression is steady and attentive, a little concerned but not alarmed — the face of someone who has just understood what is wearing his best person down and is thinking it through with her, not rushing to fix it.

## Concept 1 — The Evidence

**The artifact:** The end-of-day desk. Steering four agent sessions produces a fan of half-finished, paused work on screen and almost nothing physical to keep. The one thing she actually built by hand today is a small hand-wound mechanical timer she made at the bench, sitting alone at the front edge of the desk. The contrast is presence versus absence: many lit panels of work she only steered, one small object she can hold.
**Setting:** A real office desk at dusk, warm articulated desk lamp on, a shallow arc of monitors across the back of the desk each showing a flat pale-blue blank panel (a paused agent session), and at the front edge under the lamp pool one small hand-built mechanical timer with exposed gears.
**First-second read:** She is surrounded by lit, unfinished work but the only thing she is holding is one small object — the day produced a lot and left her almost nothing to keep.
**Compositional approach:** Engineer seated center-right at the desk, monitors filling the upper background in a shallow arc, blank and flat-lit. The small hand-built timer cradled in both her hands, low and forward, inside the lamp's light — the true focal point by light and placement, not by size. Jesse standing at her left shoulder at human scale, looking down at the object with her, not at the screens.

### Full Expanded Prompt

**COMPOSITION — most important:** 16:9 single panel. A real office desk at dusk, shot from a low three-quarter angle. Negative space is kept open in the upper-left corner and along the wall plane behind the monitors.
