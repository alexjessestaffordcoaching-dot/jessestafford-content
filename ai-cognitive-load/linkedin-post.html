In 1983, Lisanne Bainbridge wrote that when you automate a job, you leave the human the hardest tasks, the ones nobody could figure out how to automate, plus a job monitoring the machine. Forty years later, that is what an AI coding assistant does to a team.

The routine work it removes was not just filler. Small tickets, a simple test, boilerplate: those were the low-stakes stretches where a brain got to coast between hard problems.

Take them away and the day does not get shorter, it gets denser. More decisions per hour, more new problems back to back, and a new job checking work you did not write.

One of my own teams picked up these tools this year. Output went up, and so did the tiredness. Everyone said they felt faster, and they were right, which is why the strain was so hard to place.

The tool took the easy work and left your team the hard part. What should a leader be watching instead?
