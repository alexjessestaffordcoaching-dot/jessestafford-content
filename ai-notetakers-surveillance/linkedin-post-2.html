I've seen a direct report glance at the AI notetaker's recording dot, then give a thirty-second answer to a question that usually gets three minutes. It was an open question about how things were really going, and the leader I was working with had turned the tool on without ever saying where the notes went.

I don't read that as hiding. It's the small kind of fear that makes people pick the safe answer, and when nobody says who reads the notes, they fill in the gap themselves with whoever could make it career-limiting. I want to know who has information about me. Your team does too.

If you use a tool like this, say it before the meeting starts: who can see the notes, how long they are kept, and what they will and will not be used for.

I can't promise that makes every answer longer. It does take away one thing they were guessing at.

If your direct report had to guess who reads the notes from your last 1-on-1, who would they say?
